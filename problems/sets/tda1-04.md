# tda1-04 — The structure theorem: interval decomposition and barcodes

**Module:** TDA I · **Unit:** tda1-04
**Sources:** Oudot, *Persistence Theory: From Quiver Representations to Data
Analysis* — Chapter 1 "Algebraic Persistence" (pp. 13–28). §1: Theorem 1.1
(Krull–Remak–Schmidt) for finite quivers; Theorem 1.2 (Gabriel for $A_n$-type
quivers) with the interval representation $\mathbb{I}_\mathbb{Q}[b,d]$ and the
remark that this collection of intervals "is at the origin of our persistence
barcodes", together with the statement of the two limitations of Theorems 1.1
and 1.2 and the Dynkin/tame/wild trichotomy in footnote 1 (p. 17); the path
algebra and Theorem 1.3 (Auslander, Ringel, Tachikawa, Gabriel, Azumaya) for
infinite-dimensional representations (p. 17); the quivers $\mathbb{N}$ and
$\mathbb{Z}$, pointwise finite-dimensionality, Theorem 1.4 (Webb), the
equivalence of categories with $\mathbb{Z}$-graded $\boldsymbol{k}[t]$-modules
and the remark that Theorem 1.4 "can be viewed as a generalization of the
classical structure theorem for finitely generated (graded) modules over a
(graded) principal ideal domain", and Example 1.5 showing the
pointwise-finite-dimensionality hypothesis cannot be dropped (p. 18);
Theorem 1.6 (Crawley-Boevey) for arbitrary subposets of $(\mathbb{R}, \le)$ (p. 19).
§2: Definition 1.7, Definition 1.8, interval modules, and Theorem 1.9 with both
cases and the uniqueness clause, together with the "so, concretely" restatement
and the definition of interval-decomposable (pp. 20–21). §3: decorated real
numbers and the interval dictionary, Rule 1.10, the decomposition (1.12), the
persistence barcode as a multiset of intervals, the decorated and undecorated
diagrams (1.13) and (1.14) with Figure 1.1, Example 1.11 with Figure 1.2, and
the persistence measure (1.15)–(1.17) with Figures 1.3 and 1.4 (pp. 21–23).
§4: Definition 1.12 of $\mathfrak{q}$-tameness and Theorem 1.13 (p. 25). §5, the
historical discussion: that Zomorodian and Carlsson "used the structure theorem
for finitely generated modules over a principal ideal domain as decomposition
theorem for finite-dimensional persistence modules over $\mathbb{N}$" (p. 27),
the subsequent Webb and Crawley-Boevey extensions, and the comparison of
$\mathfrak{q}$-tameness with interval-decomposability with an example in each
direction (p. 28).

**What this unit must get right.** The mission strip says the barcode rests on
aa-20's structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a PID. Oudot
mentions that theorem exactly twice and neither mention proves
Theorem 1.9. Problem 5 audits the claim in three parts, and the answer is neither "yes"
nor "no".

Interleaves aa-20, whose structure theorem is the thing being audited; tda1-03,
which built the module and got a pairing out of an algorithm without ever
invoking a decomposition theorem; and cat-07, which read Definition 1.7 and
Theorem 1.9 first.

Submit your written solutions via `/grade tda1-04`.

## Problem 1 (easy — the chain of theorems, and what each one adds)

(a) State Theorem 1.1 and Theorem 1.2. Say precisely what each contributes: one
gives a decomposition into *something*, the other identifies the something.
(b) Write out the interval representation $\mathbb{I}_\mathbb{Q}[b,d]$ of an
$A_n$-type quiver as Oudot draws it, and verify that it is indecomposable.
(c) Oudot says Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 "are limited in two ways for our purposes".
Name both limitations and, for each, name the later theorem in §1 that removes
it.
(d) State Theorem 1.3 and say which of the two limitations it addresses. What is
the path algebra $\boldsymbol{k}\mathbb{Q}$, and why does it being an Artin
algebra matter?
(e) Footnote 1 gives a trichotomy: Dynkin, *tame*, and *wild* quivers. State it.
Then note that "tame" will be used in a completely different sense in §4 and say
what each of the two senses attaches to — the two are unrelated, and this unit's
mission strip uses the word.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(a) Krull–Remak–Schmidt gives indecomposables; Gabriel says what they are.
(c) One limitation is about the index set, one about dimension.
(e) One "tame" is a property of a quiver; the other of a module.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(a) Theorem 1.1 says every finite-dimensional representation of a finite quiver
decomposes as a direct sum of indecomposables, uniquely up to isomorphism and
permutation. Theorem 1.2 says that for an $A_n$-type quiver the indecomposables
are exactly the interval representations. ⟨your step: say why neither alone
gives a barcode, and what the two together give.⟩
(e) Dynkin quivers have a finite classification (Gabriel); tame quivers have a
harder but feasible one; wild quivers are hopeless. ⟨your step: state the §4
sense of "tame" and say what an author risks by using the bare word.⟩
</details>

$\mathbb{Z}$, with an example.
(b) Work Example 1.5 in full. Describe $V_i$ for $i \ge 0$ and for $i < 0$,
justify each of the four steps Oudot takes to constrain a hypothetical interval
decomposition, and state the contradiction.
(c) Which hypothesis of Theorem 1.4 does Example 1.5 violate? Say what the
example proves and what it does not — in particular whether it shows the module
is indecomposable.
(d) State Theorem 1.6 and Theorem 1.9. Show that Theorem 1.9 (ii) is the same
statement as Theorem 1.6, and that Theorem 1.9 (i) follows from Theorems 1.1,
1.2 and 1.3.
(e) Theorem 1.9's uniqueness clause reads "unique up to isomorphism and
permutation of the terms in the direct sum". Both qualifications are needed. Give
an interval-decomposable module and two decompositions of it differing only by
permutation, and say what would go wrong if uniqueness were claimed on the nose.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>

(c) Read the conclusion of the example: what is $V_0$'s dimension, and what

</details>

## Problem 5 (hard — the audit)

The mission strip for this unit says: "aa-20 (f.g. modules over a PID) is the
algebra the barcode rests on: a tame persistence module over k[t] decomposes
into intervals by exactly that theorem — the summit link of the degree."

Oudot mentions the PID structure theorem exactly twice: once on p. 18, after
Theorem 1.4, and once on p. 27 in the historical discussion. Locate both.

(a) **The $\boldsymbol{k}[t]$ claim.** Oudot states an equivalence of categories
between representations of $\mathbb{Z}$ and $\mathbb{Z}$-graded
$\boldsymbol{k}[t]$-modules, and says Webb's proof works in the latter category.
State what that equivalence is and what index set it is about. Then say for which
index sets the phrase "persistence module over $\boldsymbol{k}[t]$" makes sense
at all.
(b) **The "by exactly that theorem" claim.** Oudot writes that Theorem 1.4 "can
be viewed as a generalization of the classical structure theorem for finitely
generated (graded) modules over a (graded) principal ideal domain". Distinguish
*generalization of* from *proved by*. Then say what has to be dropped from
aa-20's hypotheses to get Theorem 1.4, and use Example 1.5 to show that
something must be put back in its place.
(c) **Where the strip is right.** On p. 27 Oudot says Zomorodian and Carlsson
"used the structure theorem for finitely generated modules over a principal
ideal domain as decomposition theorem for finite-dimensional persistence modules
over $\mathbb{N}$". State the exact class of modules for which the strip's claim
is literally true, and check it against this unit's hook, which says "for finite
type, aa-20 is why".
(d) **The word "tame".** The strip says "a tame persistence module". Oudot's §4
defines $\mathfrak{q}$-tame (Definition 1.12); footnote 1 on p. 17 uses "tame"
for a class of quivers. Determine whether the PID theorem covers
$\mathfrak{q}$-tame modules, and say what happens to the strip's claim on the
reading that "tame" means $\mathfrak{q}$-tame.
(e) Now write the audit. In one paragraph, say what the strip gets right, what
it overstates, and what a corrected version would say. Then answer the harder
question: tda1-03 obtained a pairing from an algorithm and never invoked any
decomposition theorem. Given that, what does the barcode's status as a *theorem
with a hypothesis* actually buy you that the algorithm's output does not?

(c) Finite-dimensional, over $\mathbb{N}$.
(e) The algorithm runs on a finite filtration. What happens over $\mathbb{R}$?
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>

<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) The equivalence is between $\operatorname{Rep}_{\boldsymbol{k}}(\mathbb{Z})$
— representations of the quiver $\mathbb{Z}$, equivalently persistence modules
over the poset $(\mathbb{Z}, \le)$ — and the category of $\mathbb{Z}$-graded
modules over the polynomial ring $\boldsymbol{k}[t]$. Under it, the action of $t$
is the map from each graded piece to the next, which is precisely the internal
map $v_i^{i+1}$ of the module.

That last sentence is where the index set is decided. The action of $t$ raises
the grading by *one*, so the equivalence needs an index set in which "the next
index" exists: $\mathbb{Z}$, or $\mathbb{N}$, or any $T$ order-isomorphic to
one of them. Over $T = \mathbb{R}$ there is no next index and no such action, so
"persistence module over $\boldsymbol{k}[t]$" does not name anything.

⟨your step: this is also the honest form of tda1-03's complaint about "one map
per step". Say how the two are the same observation, then do (b).⟩
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
The temptation in (e) is to score the strip. Don't. The strip names a real and
correct connection — Zomorodian and Carlsson really did prove the finite-type
barcode with aa-20 — and then attaches it to a class of modules aa-20 cannot
reach. The interesting output of the audit is the *boundary*: the exact index
set and dimension hypothesis at which the PID argument stops working and Webb's
has to take over. Find that boundary and the rest of the answer writes itself.
</details>
