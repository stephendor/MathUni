# tda1-08 — Morse theory and sublevel-set persistence

**Module:** TDA I · **Unit:** tda1-08
**Sources:** Edelsbrunner and Harer, *Computational Topology: An Introduction* —
Chapter VI "Morse Functions". §VI.1: smooth manifolds and tangent spaces,
regular and critical points via $\mathrm{D}f_x$, critical and regular values,
the Hessian, non-degeneracy, the monkey saddle as a degenerate example
(pp. 151–152); the Morse Lemma, the index of a non-degenerate critical point,
the classification into $d+1$ types, the isolation of non-degenerate critical
points, and the definition of a Morse function with both conditions (p. 152);
gradient vector fields, the Riemannian metric, the 1-parameter group of
diffeomorphisms and the conclusion that $\mathbb{M}_a$ is diffeomorphic to
$\mathbb{M}_b$ when $f^{-1}[a,b]$ is compact and critical-point-free, and the
attachment of a $q$-handle when it contains one critical point of index $q$
(pp. 153–154). §VI.2: the Morse Inequalities, weak and strong, with the
observation that the strong inequality at $j = d$ is an equality and the
derivation of the weak from the strong (p. 159). §VI.3: PL functions by linear
extension, genericity, the full subcomplexes $K_i$, the lower star, the lower
star filtration, and the deformation retraction proving $|K|_a \simeq |K_i|$
for $f(u_i) \le a < f(u_{i+1})$ (pp. 162–163); the lower link, the
classification of vertices by the reduced Betti numbers of the lower link with
Table VI.1, PL regular and simple PL critical vertices, and the definition of a
PL Morse function (pp. 163–164); unfolding, and the statement that "in contrast
to the smooth case, PL Morse functions are not dense among the class of all PL
functions" (p. 164); the PL Morse Inequalities with the Mayer–Vietoris proof
(p. 166).
Chapter VII: the filtration of a monotonic function and the persistent homology
groups (§VII.1, pp. 179–180), and the naming of "the lower star filtration of a
piecewise linear function in Section VI.3" as an example of a filtration
(p. 179).

**The contrast to keep in view.** In the smooth case Morse functions are
generic — almost every height function on a torus is one. In the PL case they
are not: Edelsbrunner says so flatly, and gives the PL monkey saddle as a
function that needs a substantial perturbation before it becomes PL Morse.
Problem 4 works out what that costs.

Interleaves an-12, whose partial derivatives and second-derivative tests are
what the Hessian is; at1-07, whose Corollary 2.11 is what converts
Edelsbrunner's homotopy equivalence into a statement about homology; at1-09,
whose Mayer–Vietoris sequence proves the PL Morse inequalities; tda1-01, whose
Definition 2.1 the lower star filtration instantiates; and tda1-03, whose
machinery turns it into a diagram.

## Problem 1 (easy — critical points, from the derivative up)

(a) Define regular and critical points of $f : \mathbb{M} \to \mathbb{R}$ via
the derivative $\mathrm{D}f_x$. Edelsbrunner notes that the image of such a map
is "either the entire line or just zero" — say why, and why that dichotomy is
what makes the two definitions exhaustive.
(b) Write the local coordinate criterion for criticality, and define the Hessian
and non-degeneracy. Connect both to an-12: which derivative test is the
one-variable shadow of non-degeneracy?
(c) Show that $x_1 = 0$ is a degenerate critical point of $f(x_1) = x_1^3$, and
that $(0,0)$ is a degenerate critical point of $f(x_1, x_2) = x_1^3 - 3x_1x_2^2$.
For the second, explain the name *monkey saddle* by describing the sign of $f$
around the origin.
(d) State the Morse Lemma. Define the index, and say why the lemma implies
non-degenerate critical points are isolated — and hence that a Morse function on
a compact manifold has finitely many of them.
(e) State the definition of a Morse function, both conditions. Then work
Edelsbrunner's torus examples: for which directions does the height function on
a geometrically perfect torus fail condition (i), and for which does it fail
condition (ii)? Give the count of exceptional directions he reports for each.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(a) A linear map to $\mathbb{R}$ has rank 0 or 1.
(b) The second-derivative test asks whether $f''(x) \ne 0$.
(e) He says two directions for (i) and two circles of directions for (ii).
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>

so the $1\times1$ Hessian is singular. ⟨your step: do the two-variable case,

(d) In the Morse Lemma's coordinates, $f(x) - f(u)$ is a non-degenerate quadratic

</details>

## Problem 2 (medium — the two structural theorems, and the inequalities)

(a) Define a vector field, the directional derivative $X[f]$, and the gradient
$\nabla f$ by the characterisation $\langle X(x), \nabla f(x)\rangle = X[f]$.
Say what a Riemannian metric is for, and why the gradient is not defined without
one.
(b) State the no-change result: if $f^{-1}[a,b]$ is compact and contains no
critical points, then $\mathbb{M}_a$ is diffeomorphic to $\mathbb{M}_b$. Say
which property of the 1-parameter group of diffeomorphisms is doing the work,
and what would go wrong if $f^{-1}[a,b]$ were not compact.
(c) State the handle-attachment result. Define what it means to attach a
$q$-handle, and say what happens in the special case $q = 0$ — Edelsbrunner
calls it "a bit different", so say exactly how.
(d) Work the torus of Figure VI.1 through the four critical points $u, v, w, z$,
naming the homotopy type of the sublevel set in each of the three intermediate
stages and which handle attachment produces it.
(e) State the weak and strong Morse Inequalities. Reproduce Edelsbrunner's
derivation of the weak from the strong, and explain why the strong inequality at
$j = d$ is an *equality* — name the invariant both sides then compute.

(e) The alternating sum of Betti numbers is tda1-02's Euler–Poincaré Theorem.

## Problem 3 (medium — the lower star filtration)

(a) Define a PL function by linear extension, and *generic*. Define the full
subcomplex $K_i$ and prove that $\sigma \in K_i$ iff every vertex of $\sigma$
has index at most $i$.
(b) Define the lower star $\mathrm{St}_- u_i$. Prove that under genericity the
lower stars *partition* $K$, and that $K_i$ is the union of the first $i$ of
them. Which of the two facts needs genericity, and which does not?
(c) Prove that $\emptyset = K_0 \subseteq K_1 \subseteq \dots \subseteq K_n = K$
is a filtration in the sense of tda1-01's Definition 2.1 — check every clause,
including the two that "filtration *of* $K$" adds.
(d) Reproduce the deformation retraction showing $|K|_a \simeq |K_i|$ for
$f(u_i) \le a < f(u_{i+1})$: write a partial simplex as a join, describe the
line segments, and give the retraction $(1-t)y + tx$ explicitly.
(e) That is a statement about *homotopy type*. Say exactly what further fact is
needed to conclude that $\mathsf{H}_*(|K|_a) \cong \mathsf{H}_*(K_i)$, name it
(it is at1-07's), and say why the persistence of the lower star filtration
therefore computes the sublevel-set persistence of $f$ rather than merely
resembling it.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(b) Each simplex has a unique maximum vertex — why?
(d) The join of the maximal face in $K_i$ and the maximal face outside it.
(e) Homotopy equivalences induce isomorphisms on homology.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(c) Nesting is immediate from the definition of $K_i$. For "filtration of $K$":
the union over all $i$ is $K$ since $K_n = K$, and the intersection is $K_0 =
\emptyset$. ⟨your step: check the *simplicial* clause too — is $K_i$ a
subcomplex of $K_j$, not merely a subset?⟩
(e) Corollary 2.11 (Hatcher, at1-07). ⟨your step: say why the argument needs the
maps as well as the objects — the sublevel sets and the $K_i$ must agree
compatibly with the inclusions, not just one index at a time.⟩
</details>

## Problem 4 (hard — PL critical points, and a genericity that fails)

(a) Define the lower link $\mathrm{Lk}_- u_i$ and give both of Edelsbrunner's
descriptions of it. Say what is attached when passing from $K_{i-1}$ to $K_i$,
and along what.
(b) Assume $K$ triangulates a $d$-manifold. State the two consequences
Edelsbrunner draws about vertex stars and vertex links, and say how they
"restrict the possibilities dramatically".
(c) Reproduce Table VI.1 for a 2-manifold: the reduced Betti numbers of the
lower link for a regular vertex, a minimum, a saddle and a maximum. For each,
say what the lower link *is* geometrically and check the Betti numbers against
it. Take care over the empty lower link, which Edelsbrunner flags as the
exception to $\tilde\beta_0 = \beta_0 - 1$.
(d) Define a PL regular vertex, a simple PL critical vertex of index $q$, and a
PL Morse function. Note that "PL regular" is *not* "lower link empty" — state
the difference and say which of the four rows of Table VI.1 it distinguishes.
(e) Now the contrast. Edelsbrunner writes: "In contrast to the smooth case, PL
Morse functions are not dense among the class of all PL functions. Equivalently,
a PL function on a manifold may require a substantial perturbation before it
becomes PL Morse."

  First: say what "dense" would mean here and what its failure costs — in
  particular, whether one may assume a PL Morse function as input.

  Second: work the PL monkey saddle. Define a $k$-fold saddle by
  $\tilde\beta_0 = k$ and describe the unfolding into $k$ simple saddles: how
  many new vertices, and what function values?

  Third: unfolding changes the triangulation. Say what would have to be checked
  before concluding that the unfolded function has the same persistence
  diagram as the original, and whether anything in this unit's sources
  establishes it.

(d) One row has an empty lower link and one has a non-empty but trivial one.
(e) Third: two functions on two different complexes.
</details>

## Problem 5 (hard — from a scalar field to a barcode)

(a) Assemble the chain. Starting from a PL function $f$ on a triangulated
manifold, list every step needed to produce a persistence diagram, naming the
unit and the result that licenses each. Then say which single step is *not* in
this unit's sources.
(b) The lower star filtration is indexed by vertices; tda1-03's filtration comes
from a monotonic $f : K \to \mathbb{R}$ on *simplices*. Define the monotonic
function whose filtration is the lower star filtration, prove it monotonic, and
check that its compatible orderings are exactly the orderings by lower star.
(c) State the PL Morse Inequalities and reproduce the inductive step of their
proof: the Mayer–Vietoris sequence with $K = K_i$, $K' = K_{i-1}$,
$K'' = \mathrm{St}_- u_i \cup \mathrm{Lk}_- u_i$ and $A = \mathrm{Lk}_- u_i$.
Say why $K''$ is homologically trivial and derive
$\operatorname{rank}\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_p(K_i) = \operatorname{rank}
\tilde{\mathsf{H}}_p(K_{i-1}) - k^p + k_{p-1}$.
(d) Compare the two sets of inequalities. The smooth version counts critical
points of a Morse function; the PL version counts PL critical vertices.
Edelsbrunner "omit[s] the proof of the strong inequalities and instead refer[s]
to the proof of their PL versions". Say what that tells you about which of the
two settings is the more elementary, and whether the PL proof establishes the
smooth statement.
(e) The reflection. This unit's mission strip says: "Sublevel-set persistence
links Morse theory to filtrations — how a scalar field becomes a barcode."

  First: audit the link. Which direction does it run, and what is the precise
  bridge — name the sentence in Chapter VII that names the lower star filtration
  and say what it licenses.

  Second: the hook says "topology only changes at critical points". That is the
  smooth statement. Say what the PL analogue is, why it is a different claim,
  and what happens to the hook's sentence for a PL function that is not PL
  Morse.

  Third: "how a scalar field becomes a barcode". A scalar field measured on data
  is not a PL function on a triangulated manifold. List what must be supplied to
  get from one to the other, and say for each whether it appears in this unit's
  sources.
