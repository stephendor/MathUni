Lindström.** The syllabus gives "Oxford M2 notes (multivariable analysis)"; that

**One word in Definition 6.1.1 is the whole of an2-07.** Lindström's derivative is
"a **bounded**, linear map $A : X \to Y$" such that the error goes to zero faster
than $r$. In finite dimensions the word is free — every linear map on
$\mathbf{R}^n$ is bounded — and in general it is load-bearing: he says so himself,
"the assumption that $\mathbf{F}'(\mathbf{a})$ is bounded turns out to be
essential". Problem 1(e) is what it buys, and the answer is **Theorem 5.4.5**.

## Problem 2 (the chain rule becomes composition)

(a) State **Theorem 6.1.9**, the chain rule, and note the shape of its conclusion:
$(\mathbf{F}\circ\mathbf{G})'(\mathbf{a}) = \mathbf{F}'(\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{a}))
\circ \mathbf{G}'(\mathbf{a})$ — a **composition of linear maps**, where the
one-variable rule has a product of numbers. Explain why the two statements are the
same statement, using your answer to Problem 1(a).

(b) Prove the chain rule. The proof estimates the error term of the composite in
terms of the two error terms; write it out and say where boundedness of each
derivative is used.

(c) Prove **Proposition 6.1.8**: for $\alpha : O \to \mathbb{K}$ and
$\mathbf{F} : O \to Y$ both differentiable at $\mathbf{a}$, the product
$\alpha\mathbf{F}$ is differentiable with the expected formula. Then do
Lindström's Exercise 4 to §6.1: if $A : Y \to Z$ is a **bounded linear** map and
$\mathbf{G}$ is differentiable, then $A \circ \mathbf{G}$ is differentiable with
$(A\circ\mathbf{G})' = A \circ \mathbf{G}'$. Say why this is a special case of the
chain rule, and which proposition supplies $A' $.

(d) Do Lindström's Exercise 2 to §6.1: a map $\mathbf{F}(\mathbf{x}) =
A(\mathbf{x}) + \mathbf{c}$ with $A$ bounded linear is *affine*; find its
derivative at every point, and say which two of Propositions 6.1.5, 6.1.6, 6.1.7
you used.

(e) A structural question. For each $\mathbf{a} \in O$ the derivative
$\mathbf{F}'(\mathbf{a})$ is an element of $\mathcal{L}(X,Y)$ — an2-07's
**Definition 5.4.6** — so $\mathbf{F}'$ is itself a map $O \to \mathcal{L}(X,Y)$.
Using **Theorem 5.4.8**, say when that codomain is a Banach space, and what
hypothesis on $X$ is needed. Then say what "$\mathbf{F}'$ is continuous at
$\mathbf{a}$" means, and in which norm.

<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
(a) In one variable, $X = Y = Z = \mathbf{R}$ and every linear map

"$\mathbf{F}'$ continuous at $\mathbf{a}$" means continuity in the **operator
