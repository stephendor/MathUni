# tda1-09 — The persistence algorithm and duality

**Module:** TDA I · **Unit:** tda1-09
**Sources:** Edelsbrunner and Harer, *Computational Topology: An Introduction* —
§VII.2 "Efficient Implementations" (pp. 186–191): the sparse matrix
representation by a linear array of linked lists, the migration of lists from
right to left, the TOP operation and occupied entries, and the pseudocode
(p. 186); list addition by parallel scan, the analysis by *collisions*, the
monotone right-to-left behaviour of the inner loop, the containment of
collisions in the interval $[i, j]$, and the bound $(p+1)(j-i)^2$ per simplex
with the output-sensitive refinement (p. 187); the zeroth diagram, the two cases
for an edge, the reduction to union–find and the $m\alpha(m)$ bound
(pp. 188–189); surfaces, the two questions, the identification of the lower star
filtration with the filtration of $g(\sigma) = \max_{x \in \sigma} f(x)$, and the
commuting square of isomorphisms (p. 189); the Persistence Equivalence Theorem
(p. 190); the first diagram obtained by reflecting the finite part of
$\mathrm{Dgm}_0(-f)$ across the minor diagonal, with Figure VII.8, the
three-way partition of the edges giving the points at infinity, and the
three-pass $m\alpha(m)$ algorithm for a PL function on a triangulated
2-manifold (pp. 190–191).
§IV.4 "Exact Sequences" (pp. 114–118): chain complexes and chain maps freed from
the simplicial setting, exactness, short exact sequences of chain complexes and
what they imply pointwise (p. 114); the Snake Lemma, the derivation of the exact
homology sequence of a pair from it, and the four-step construction of the
connecting homomorphism (pp. 115–117); the Mayer–Vietoris Sequence Theorem with
its proof (p. 117).

**What these pages do not contain.** No cohomology — that is Chapter V, not this
unit's resource — and therefore no cohomological persistence algorithm and no
Ripser. Edelsbrunner's speed-up is union–find plus a reflection. The duality he
uses for it is stated in §VII.3, which tda1-05 read; §VII.2 invokes it and says
so. Problem 5 sorts out which parts of the hook and the strip these pages
support.

Interleaves tda1-03, which proved the algorithm *correct* — the Pairing Lemma —
where this unit makes it fast; at1-07's Corollary 2.11, which underwrites the
vertical isomorphisms of p. 189; at1-09, whose long exact sequences the Snake
Lemma manufactures; tda1-08, whose lower star filtration is the input; and
lab-03.

## Problem 1 (easy — the sparse implementation)

(a) Describe the sparse matrix representation: the array $\partial[1..m]$, the
linked list per entry, the sorted order, and what the list in entry $j$ holds at
the start and what the list in entry $j$ holds at the end. Explain the sentence
"we see a general migration of the lists from right to left".
(b) Write out the pseudocode and match each line to a line of tda1-03's dense
algorithm. Which quantity plays the role of $low(j)$?
(c) Explain why adding two lists is a merge that deletes both copies of every
duplicate, and why sorted order makes it a parallel scan. Which fact about the
coefficients is being used?
(d) Define a *collision*. Say what it means for the loop to end with $L$ empty
and what it means for it to end with a successful deposit, and connect both to
tda1-03's positive and negative simplices.
(e) Show that the inner loop proceeds monotonically from right to left. The
argument is one sentence about $\sigma_k$ being deleted — reproduce it and say
where modulo 2 arithmetic enters.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(b) The array index where a list finally lands.
(c) $1 + 1 = 0$.
(e) $\sigma_k$ is topmost in both lists being merged.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(e) When $L$ collides with $R[k]$, the simplex $\sigma_k$ is topmost in both, so
in the merge it cancels. ⟨your step: conclude that the topmost simplex of the

## Problem 2 (medium — the analysis)

(a) Show that collisions for $\sigma_j$ happen only at entries between $i$ and
$j$, where $i = 1$ if $\sigma_j$ gives birth and $i$ is the index of the
corresponding birth if $\sigma_j$ gives death. Identify $j - i$ in the second
case in the vocabulary of tda1-03.
(b) Reproduce the nesting argument: a collision at entry $k$ can happen only if
$\sigma_k$ gave birth to a class that died at some $\sigma_l$ with
$i < k < l < j$. Then explain the inductive step that concludes the lists added
at collisions contain only faces of simplices with index in $[i, j]$.
(c) Derive the bound $(p+1)(j-i)^2$ on the running time of the inner loop for a
$p$-simplex $\sigma_j$. Say where the factor $p+1$ comes from and where the
square comes from.
(d) State the output-sensitive refinement: what does knowing in advance which
simplices give birth buy, and what is the resulting bound? Edelsbrunner says it
is "at most proportional to $m^3$ but for most practical data it is
significantly smaller" — say what property of the data makes it smaller, in
terms of the quantity that appears in the bound.
(e) Compare with tda1-02's Smith normal form analysis, which gave a constant
times $2n_{p-1}n_p\min\{n_{p-1},n_p\}$ per dimension. Both are cubic in the
worst case. Say what is genuinely different about the two bounds and which of
the two can be beaten by favourable input.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(c) How many faces does a $p$-simplex have in each dimension, and how many
indices are in $[i,j]$?
(d) The bound is a sum of squares.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(c) The number of faces of simplices with index in $[i, j]$ is at most $p+1$
times the number of indices in the interval, so a merge costs at most
$(p+1)(j-i)$. ⟨your step: multiply by the number of collisions, which is also
bounded by the length of the interval.⟩
(d) The bound becomes the dimension times the sum of squares of the index
persistences. ⟨your step: say what a data set with many short bars does to that
sum, and contrast with one long bar.⟩
</details>

## Problem 3 (medium — dimension zero, and why it collapses)

(a) Explain why the 0-th diagram "depends solely on the vertices and edges of
$K$ and on their sequence in the compatible ordering".
(b) Work the edge case in full. For $\partial\sigma_j = u + w$ with $u = \sigma_i$
first and $w = \sigma_k$ second, describe the deposit attempt at $R[k]$, the two
outcomes, and why every list involved has length exactly two. Conclude the
$m^2$ bound.
(c) Now the improvement. Show that $\sigma_j$ gives birth iff $u$ and $w$ lie in
the same component of $K_{j-1}$, and gives death otherwise. Describe what the
growing path is in each case, and why in the death case the inner loop ends at
the *oldest* vertex of one component.
(d) Edelsbrunner writes that "following the Elder Rule, $L$ gets deposited in
$R[i]$". Say exactly where the Elder Rule enters, and why the monotone
right-to-left behaviour of the inner loop is what makes the older of the two
components the survivor.
(e) Describe the union–find reduction: what is stored, what is queried, why using
the index of the oldest vertex as the component name is the right choice, and
what the $m\alpha(m)$ bound is. Then say why the same trick does not obviously
work in dimension one.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(b) A vertex has empty boundary; an edge has two vertices.
(d) Which component's oldest vertex is older?
(e) $\alpha$ is the inverse Ackermann function.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(c) If $u$ and $w$ are already connected in $K_{j-1}$ then adding $\sigma_j$
completes a cycle. ⟨your step: describe the growing path the algorithm builds,
say what $L$ tracks, and say what happens to $L$ in each of the two cases.⟩
(e) In dimension one there is no analogue of a component to merge — the relevant
classes are 1-cycles, and no near-linear structure tracks them. ⟨your step: say
what Edelsbrunner does instead, which is the subject of Problem 4.⟩
</details>

## Problem 4 (medium-hard — the reflection, and what makes it legal)

(a) State the two questions Edelsbrunner poses for surfaces. Answer the first:
give the monotonic $g$ whose filtration is the lower star filtration, and say
why $g(\sigma) = \max_{x\in\sigma} f(x)$ is the right choice.
(b) Write out the commuting square relating $\mathsf{H}_p(K_a) \to
\mathsf{H}_p(K_b)$ to $\mathsf{H}_p(|K|_a) \to \mathsf{H}_p(|K|_b)$. Say why the
vertical maps are isomorphisms — name the result from tda1-08 and the result
from at1-07 that together give it — and why the square commutes.
(c) State the Persistence Equivalence Theorem and apply it to conclude that the
diagrams of $f$ and $g$ agree. Say precisely which two hypotheses of the theorem
parts (a) and (b) have just verified.
(d) Now the second question. Derive the relation between the finite parts of
$\mathrm{Dgm}_1(f)$ and $\mathrm{Dgm}_0(-f)$: follow Edelsbrunner's reasoning
that "looking at $-f$ is like taking the complement and going backward", and
show that $(a,b) \in \mathrm{Dgm}_1(f)$ iff $(-b,-a) \in \mathrm{Dgm}_0(-f)$.
Identify the reflection as one across the minor diagonal.
(e) The infinite points are not obtained by reflection. Give the three-way
partition of the edges of $K$, say what each part contributes, and explain why
the third part gives points at infinity. Then state the running time of the
resulting three-pass algorithm and say which pass is which.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(b) A homotopy equivalence of inclusions, plus homotopy invariance of homology.
(d) A sublevel set of $-f$ is a superlevel set of $f$.
(e) Edges that give death for $f$, edges that give death for $-f$, and the rest.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(d) A point $(a,b)$ of $\mathrm{Dgm}_1(f)$ marks the birth of a 1-class at $a$
and its death at $b$. Passing to $-f$ reverses the order and negates the values,
so the same event becomes the birth of a 0-class at $-b$ and its death at $-a$.
⟨your step: state the equivalence and identify the map $(a,b)\mapsto(-b,-a)$.⟩
(e) The three parts: edges giving death in the lower star filtration of $f$,
edges giving death in the lower star filtration of $-f$, and the rest. ⟨your
step: say which diagram each of the first two contributes to, and why an edge in
neither gives a class that never dies.⟩
</details>

## Problem 5 (hard — exact sequences, and the audit)

(a) Define a chain complex and a chain map in Edelsbrunner's setting-free form.
Show that a chain map sends cycles to cycles and boundaries to boundaries, and
hence induces a map on homology. Compare with tda1-02's $f_\#$ — and note that
this section states the containments correctly where §IV.1 did not.
(b) Define exactness at $\mathcal{V}$ and a short exact sequence of chain
complexes. State the three consequences Edelsbrunner draws pointwise, and say
why the isomorphism $\mathsf{V}_p \cong \mathsf{U}_p \oplus \mathsf{W}_p$ has
"no natural choice".
(c) State the Snake Lemma. Then derive the exact homology sequence of a pair
from it: exhibit the short exact sequence of chain complexes, verify injectivity,
surjectivity, and exactness in the middle.
(d) Construct the connecting homomorphism $D$ in Edelsbrunner's four steps.
Steps 3 and 4 are the well-definedness checks — say what is being chosen in each
and what has to be shown, and say why the construction would be worthless
without them.
(e) The audit, in three parts.

  First: the hook says "with its clever pairing and cohomological speed-up — why
  Ripser (lab-03) is so fast". Search both sources for cohomology and report
  what you find. Name the chapter where cohomology is defined, say whether it is
  in this unit's resources, and say what Edelsbrunner's actual speed-up is.

  Second: the strip says "the reduction algorithm's correctness and the duality
  that makes it efficient". Correctness was established in tda1-03, by the
  Pairing Lemma; say what §VII.2 adds and what it does not. Then find the
  duality that genuinely does make the algorithm efficient here, name where it is
  stated, and say why it earns the word.

  Third: write the corrected hook and strip. They should claim the speed-up
  Edelsbrunner actually has, attribute the duality to the section that proves
  it, and not attribute to these pages an algorithm they do not contain.
