# tda1-03 — Persistent homology and the persistence module

**Module:** TDA I · **Unit:** tda1-03
**Sources:** Edelsbrunner and Harer, *Computational Topology: An Introduction* —
§VII.1 "Persistent Homology" (pp. 178–185): the merge tree of a continuous
function and the Elder Rule, with $\beta(a,b)$ counted as the number of
spanning paths and the uniqueness claim for Morse $f$ (pp. 178–179); monotonic
functions on a simplicial complex, the filtration
$\emptyset = K_0 \subseteq \dots \subseteq K_n = K$, and the induced sequence of
homology groups (p. 179); the definition of the persistent homology groups
$\mathsf{H}_p^{i,j} = \operatorname{im} f_p^{i,j}$ and persistent Betti numbers,
the subquotient formula, birth, death entering, persistence and index
persistence, and the remark that births and deaths need only a finite sequence
of vector spaces and linear maps (p. 180); persistence diagrams, the
multiplicity $\mu_p^{i,j}$, the quadrant rule, and the Fundamental Lemma of
Persistent Homology (p. 181); compatible orderings, the single boundary matrix
$\partial$, the left-to-right column reduction, $R = \partial \cdot V$ and
$\beta_p = \#Zero_p - \#Low_p$ (pp. 181–182); the Pairing Lemma with its
submatrix-rank proof, positive and negative simplices, and the identification of
lowest ones with finite diagram points (pp. 183–184); the worked triangle with
its dummy $(-1)$-simplex, Figures VII.4 and VII.5 (pp. 184–185).
Oudot, *Persistence Theory: From Quiver Representations to Data Analysis* —
Chapter 1 "Algebraic Persistence": representations of $(T, \le)$ as functors to
vector spaces with the functoriality constraints (1.10), footnote 4 on the poset
category, and Theorem 1.6 (Crawley-Boevey) (p. 19); Definition 1.7 of a
persistence module, Definition 1.8 on zigzag modules with its rephrasing,
interval modules, and Theorem 1.9 with its two cases and uniqueness clause
(pp. 20–21); decorated real numbers, the interval dictionary, and Rule 1.10 on
choosing the smallest representative interval (p. 21).

**The point of the unit is that these are the same object twice.** Edelsbrunner
builds a sequence of homology groups and the images of the maps between them and
never once writes the phrase "persistence module". Oudot defines the persistence
module and never builds one from a filtration in Chapter 1. Problem 4 puts them
together and Problem 5 audits what the mission strip claims about the result.

Interleaves tda1-01, whose Definition 2.1 is the general filtration this unit's
is a special case of; tda1-02, whose reduction this unit's algorithm is a
constrained version of — and whose derived prediction is tested in Problem 3;
at1-09, whose field coefficients are what make "vector space" legitimate here;
and cat-07, which already read Definition 1.7 and Theorem 1.9.

## Problem 1 (easy — the Elder Rule, before any algebra)

(a) Let $\mathbb{X}$ be connected and $f$ continuous. Describe the merge tree
$G(f)$ and say why it is a tree rather than a general graph — which two facts
about components under increasing threshold are being used?
(b) State the Elder Rule. Then define $\beta(a,b)$ as Edelsbrunner does and
explain why $\beta(a,b)$ equals the number of paths in the decomposition that
span $[a,b]$.
(c) Edelsbrunner adds that "any path decomposition that is not generated using
the Elder Rule does not have this property". Construct a small merge tree with
two internal branch points, give a non-elder decomposition of it, and exhibit a
pair $a \le b$ for which the spanning-path count differs from $\beta(a,b)$.
(d) The uniqueness claim is stated for Morse $f$ only. Say what could go wrong
without that hypothesis, in terms of the function values at junctures.
(e) Define a *monotonic* function $f : K \to \mathbb{R}$ and prove that
monotonicity is exactly what makes each sublevel set $K(a)$ a subcomplex.
Give a non-monotonic $f$ on a triangle and say which $K(a)$ fails.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(a) Components can merge; can they split?
(d) What if two paths merge at a juncture where both have the same birth value?
(e) Subcomplex means closed under taking faces.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(a) As the threshold increases the sublevel set only grows, so components never
shrink and never split; they may merge. A graph in which arcs merge but never
split, drawn upward and ending in a single component, has no cycles. ⟨your step:
say why "ends in a single component" is needed as well as "never splits", and
where connectedness of $\mathbb{X}$ is used.⟩
(e) If $\sigma$ is a face of $\tau$ and $\tau \in K(a)$ then $f(\tau) \le a$,
and monotonicity gives $f(\sigma) \le f(\tau) \le a$, so $\sigma \in K(a)$.
⟨your step: do the converse — show that if every $K(a)$ is a subcomplex then $f$
is monotonic — and then build the counterexample.⟩
</details>

## Problem 2 (medium — the groups, and what "dies" means)

(a) Write down the sequence of homology groups induced by the filtration, name
the maps, and state the definition of $\mathsf{H}_p^{i,j}$ and
$\beta_p^{i,j}$. Verify $\mathsf{H}_p^{i,i} = \mathsf{H}_p(K_i)$.
(b) Prove the subquotient formula $\mathsf{H}_p^{i,j} = \mathsf{Z}_p(K_i) /
(\mathsf{B}_p(K_j) \cap \mathsf{Z}_p(K_i))$ from the definition as an image.
Say which isomorphism theorem you are using.
(c) Define what it means for a class $\gamma$ to be *born* at $K_i$ and to *die
entering* $K_j$. Both are stated as membership conditions on images; unpack each
into a sentence about cycles and boundaries, and say why death is defined by
merging with an older class rather than by becoming a boundary.
(d) Define persistence and index persistence. Give a filtration in which two
classes have equal index persistence and very different persistence, and say
which of the two quantities a stability theorem would have to be about.
(e) Edelsbrunner notes that births and deaths "can also be defined for a
sequence of vector spaces that are not necessarily homology groups. All we need
is a finite sequence and homomorphisms from left to right." Say precisely what
this remark gives up and what it keeps, and connect it to Definition 1.7.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(b) The image of a map $A/A' \to B/B'$ is $(A + B')/B'$.
(c) Read $\gamma \notin \mathsf{H}_p^{i-1,i}$ aloud.
(e) Which of Edelsbrunner's hypotheses does Definition 1.7 not require?
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(b) $f_p^{i,j}$ sends $z + \mathsf{B}_p(K_i)$ to $z + \mathsf{B}_p(K_j)$, so its
image is $(\mathsf{Z}_p(K_i) + \mathsf{B}_p(K_j)) / \mathsf{B}_p(K_j)$. ⟨your
step: apply the second isomorphism theorem and identify the intersection.⟩
(e) It keeps: a finite index set, maps composing left to right, and hence
births, deaths and multiplicities. It gives up: any relation to a space. ⟨your
step: compare with Definition 1.7 — which of "finite", "homology" and "left to
right" does Oudot need, and which does he drop?⟩
</details>

## Problem 3 (medium — the algorithm, and a prediction to test)

(a) Define a *compatible ordering* of the simplices and prove one exists when
$f$ is monotonic. Show that every initial subsequence forms a subcomplex, and
say why that is the property the algorithm actually uses.
(b) Write down the single boundary matrix $\partial$ over all dimensions and the
reduction algorithm. Define $low(j)$ and *reduced*. Then state exactly which
matrix operations the algorithm performs.
(c) **The test.** tda1-02 derived, from tda1-01's Definition 2.1 together with
the description of REDUCE, that a filtered reduction may only let a basis
element absorb elements of grade no larger — admitting the addition of an
earlier column to a later one, and excluding the free pivot exchange REDUCE
performs. Check that prediction against the algorithm on p. 182. Report whether it holds,
where it holds exactly and where it is looser or tighter than what the book
does, and say what the book gets to read the answer off given that a one-sided
reduction cannot produce a diagonal.
(d) Show $\beta_p = \#Zero_p(R) - \#Low_p(R)$, explaining why
$\#Zero_p(R) = \operatorname{rank} \mathsf{Z}_p$ and
$\#Low_p(R) = \operatorname{rank} \mathsf{B}_p$. Compare with tda1-02's
$\beta_p = \operatorname{rank} \mathsf{Z}_p - \operatorname{rank} \mathsf{B}_p$
and say what has and has not changed.
(e) State and prove the Pairing Lemma. The proof evaluates
$r_R(i,j) = \operatorname{rank} R_i^j - \operatorname{rank} R_{i+1}^j +
\operatorname{rank} R_{i+1}^{j-1} - \operatorname{rank} R_i^{j-1}$ in three
cases; reproduce all three, and say which step uses the fact that left-to-right
column operations preserve the rank of every lower-left submatrix.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(a) Order by function value, breaking ties by dimension.
(c) Count the operations the pseudocode performs. There is one.
(e) In a reduced matrix, a linear combination of non-zero columns is non-zero —
why?
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(c) The pseudocode's only operation is "add column $j_0$ to column $j$" with
$j_0 < j$. ⟨your step: match that against the prediction one operation at a
time, then answer the last part — the reduced matrix $R$ is not unique, so
whatever the answer is read off must be something the Pairing Lemma shows is.⟩
(e) In a reduced matrix distinct non-zero columns have distinct lowest ones, so
in any sum the column whose lowest one is lowest contributes an entry nothing
else can cancel. ⟨your step: conclude that rank equals the number of non-zero
columns for every $R_i^j$, then do the three cases.⟩
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
(c) is the point of having made the prediction in the first place. Do not settle
for "yes, confirmed". A prediction is more useful where it is *not* exact: ask
whether the algorithm is more restrictive than the constraint required, and if
so, what the extra restriction buys. The answer to the last part is the whole
reason persistence is computable in one pass.
</details>

## Problem 4 (medium-hard — the same object, twice)

(a) State Definition 1.7 and write out the functoriality constraints (1.10).
Then take Edelsbrunner's filtration and produce from it a persistence module in
exactly Oudot's sense: name $T$, name $V_i$, name $v_i^j$, and verify both
constraints.
(b) Edelsbrunner defines $\mathsf{H}_p^{i,j}$ as an image and never defines a
persistence module. Oudot defines a persistence module and never builds one from
a filtration in Chapter 1. Say precisely which of Edelsbrunner's data is
*discarded* when you pass to the module, and which of Oudot's structure
Edelsbrunner never names but does use.
(c) Show that $\beta_p^{i,j} = \operatorname{rank} v_i^j$ for the module of
part (a). Then say what an interval module $\mathbb{I}S$ is, and what
$\operatorname{rank} v_i^j$ is for it.
(d) State Theorem 1.9 with both of its cases and its uniqueness clause. Which
case applies to Edelsbrunner's filtration, and why does he never need to invoke
it? (His algorithm produces the pairing directly — say what the theorem would
add that the algorithm does not.)
(e) Oudot's Rule 1.10 fixes an ambiguity that Edelsbrunner's setting hides. Take
$T = \{1,2,3,4\}$ and $S = \{2,3\}$ and list four intervals of $\mathbb{R}$ whose
intersection with $T$ is $S$. Say why the ambiguity cannot arise for
Edelsbrunner, and what would have to change for it to.

## Problem 5 (hard — diagrams, the Fundamental Lemma, and the strip)

(a) Define $\mu_p^{i,j}$ by Edelsbrunner's formula and justify it: say what each
of the two differences counts and why the subtraction is the right correction.
(b) Define $\mathrm{Dgm}_p(f)$. Explain why all points lie strictly above the
diagonal, why the diagonal points are then added with infinite multiplicity, and
what the vertical distance to the diagonal is.
(c) State the quadrant rule for reading $\beta_p^{k,l}$ off the diagram and
derive the closed/open sidedness of the quadrant from the condition
$a_i \le a_k$ and $a_j > a_l$.
(d) State and prove the Fundamental Lemma of Persistent Homology. Then say
exactly what "the diagram encodes the entire information about persistent
homology groups" does and does not claim — in particular whether it claims the
diagram determines the filtration.
(e) The reflection. This unit's mission strip says: "The persistence module —
one vector space per level, one map per step — is the mission's central
algebraic object."

  First: "one vector space per level" is exact. "One map per step" is not, and
  Definition 1.7 says why. Write down what Definition 1.7 actually supplies, say
  for which index sets the two descriptions agree, and give an index set for
  which "step" has no meaning at all.

  Second: the strip calls it the central algebraic object. Test that against what
  this unit needed. Which results here are about the module, and which are about
  the filtration or the matrix and would survive if the module were never
  mentioned?

  Third: work the triangle example of pp. 184–185 in full — seven simplices plus
  the dummy $(-1)$-simplex, the reduction, the four pairs, and all three
  diagrams. Then say which single line of the reduced matrix is the whole of
  $\mathsf{H}_1$, and what that shows about the relation between the algorithm's
  output and the module's decomposition.
