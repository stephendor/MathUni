# tda1-05 — Persistence diagrams and their space

**Module:** TDA I · **Unit:** tda1-05
**Sources:** Oudot, *Persistence Theory: From Quiver Representations to Data
Analysis* — Chapter 1, §3 and §4: the persistence measure (1.15)–(1.17) and its
additivity, with Figures 1.3 and 1.4 (pp. 22–24); the inclusion–exclusion
formulas (1.18) relating $\mu_\mathbb{V}$ to the ranks of the internal maps,
with the quadrant, strip and rectangle patterns of Figure 1.5 and the finiteness
proviso (p. 24); §4's programme of proceeding "backwards compared to Section 3",
Definition 1.12 of $\mathfrak{q}$-tameness with the explanation of the name,
the non-negativity argument via restriction to finite index sets,
and Theorem 1.13 (p. 25); the agreement of the two definitions above the diagonal and the
possible disagreement along it, ephemeral modules, the observable category, and
the comparison of $\mathfrak{q}$-tameness with interval-decomposability
(pp. 26–28).
Edelsbrunner and Harer, *Computational Topology: An Introduction* — Chapter VII:
§VII.1's construction of $\mathrm{Dgm}_p(f)$, the addition of the diagonal with
infinite multiplicity "for technical reasons which will become clear in the next
chapter", and the quadrant rule (p. 181); §VII.2's Persistence Equivalence
Theorem and the reflection relating $\mathrm{Dgm}_1(f)$ to
$\mathrm{Dgm}_0(-f)$, with Figure VII.8 (pp. 190–191); §VII.3 on elevation, the
extended filtration through absolute and relative homology, Rules 1–3, the
genus-2 torus worked example, the ordinary, extended and relative sub-diagrams
with Figures VII.9 and VII.10, and the Persistence Duality Theorem and
Persistence Symmetry Theorem (pp. 192–195).

**What these two chapters do not contain.** Neither defines a *metric* on
diagrams. Edelsbrunner defers it explicitly — the diagonal is added for reasons
"which will become clear in the next chapter", Chapter VIII — and Oudot defers
it to his Chapter 3. Both are tda1-06's resources. So this unit defines the
objects and the one piece of structure that exists *for* a metric, and Problem 5
audits a mission strip that asserts the metric.

Interleaves tda1-04, whose barcode this unit generalises past the point where a
barcode exists; tda1-03, whose $\mu_p^{i,j}$ is the finite case of $\mu_\mathbb{V}$;
at1-09, whose relative homology and long exact sequence are what the extended
filtration runs through; and an2-01, whose metric space axioms are the checklist
Problem 5 uses.

Submit your written solutions via `/grade tda1-05`.

## Problem 1 (easy — the measure, and reading it off ranks)

(a) State the four inclusion–exclusion formulas (1.18). Say which region of the
extended plane each counts, and check that the second, third and fourth follow
from the first by inclusion–exclusion.
(b) State the finiteness proviso Oudot attaches to (1.18): all the ranks finite,
"or, less stringently, that all but rank $v_q^r$" finite. Explain why exactly
that one rank is allowed to be infinite, and what the alternating sums evaluate
to when it is.
(c) Verify (1.18) directly for the interval module $\mathbb{I}[b, d)$: compute
$\operatorname{rank} v_i^j$ for all $i < j$, and check the fourth formula
against the single point of its diagram.
(d) Prove the additivity (1.17) from (1.18) rather than from the diagram, by
cancelling the duplicated terms in the alternating sums.
(e) Oudot says these formulas "are useful to localize points in the persistence
diagram from the sole knowledge of the ranks of the linear maps". Say what is
gained by having a rank-only description, given that §3 already had a diagram.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(a) Start from the quadrant and cut it down.
(b) Which rank is the one counting a region that touches the diagonal?
(c) $\operatorname{rank} v_i^j = 1$ exactly when both $i$ and $j$ lie in
$[b, d)$.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(b) The first formula says $\operatorname{rank} v_q^r$ is the multiplicity of the
quadrant $[-\infty, q] \times [r, +\infty]$, and that region is unbounded. ⟨your
step: say why the other three ranks appear only as corrections, so their
finiteness is what makes the differences meaningful, and what the sum is when the
leading term is infinite.⟩

## Problem 2 (medium — a diagram without a decomposition)

(a) Oudot describes §4 as proceeding "backwards compared to Section 3". Set out
both directions explicitly: what §3 defines from what, and what §4 defines from
what.
(b) State Definition 1.12 and explain the name *quadrant*-tame using the first
formula of (1.18). Say precisely what is allowed at $i = j$ and what is
forbidden for $i < j$, and why forcing finiteness at $i = j$ too would give back
pointwise finite-dimensionality.
(c) Oudot notes that non-negativity of $\mu_\mathbb{V}$ "is not obvious at first
sight". Reproduce his argument: for each of the four formulas, name the finite
index set $J$ to which $\mathbb{V}$ is restricted, and say which theorem makes
the restriction interval-decomposable.
(d) State Theorem 1.13. Identify each of its three qualifications — *uniquely
defined*, *locally finite*, and *in the open extended upper half-plane* — and say
what each rules out.
(e) The two definitions of $\mathrm{Dgm}(\mathbb{V})$ agree above the diagonal
and may differ on it. Say which summands cause the disagreement, why the
rectangles of Theorem 1.13 cannot see them, and why Oudot says either definition
may be used in practice.

(c) $J = \{q, r\}$ for the first formula.
(d) What could go wrong if the multiset were allowed to accumulate?
(e) Look for summands of the form $\mathbb{I}[b, b]$.
</details>

## Problem 3 (medium — Edelsbrunner's diagrams, and one theorem that travels)

(a) State the Persistence Equivalence Theorem. Say what data it needs — draw the
ladder — and what it concludes. Then say why it is a statement about
*presentations* rather than about spaces.
(b) Use it. Edelsbrunner computes $\mathrm{Dgm}_0(f)$ for a piecewise linear $f$
by computing the 0-th diagram of a different function $g$. Explain what the
theorem licenses there, and what would have to be checked to apply it.
(c) Derive the relation $\mathrm{Dgm}_1(f) = \mathrm{Dgm}_0^R(-f)$ on finite
points, where $R$ is reflection across the minor diagonal $(a,b) \mapsto
(-b,-a)$. Follow Edelsbrunner's reasoning about complements and reversal, and
say why it is stated for the *finite* portion only.
(d) The infinite points of $\mathrm{Dgm}_1(f)$ are obtained by partitioning the
edges of the complex into three sets. Name the three and say what each
contributes.
(e) Compare Edelsbrunner's $\mathrm{Dgm}_p(f)$ with Oudot's
$\mathrm{Dgm}(\mathbb{V})$ as objects. Both are multisets in the extended plane;
list every structural difference you can find, and for each say whether it is a
difference of convention or of content.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(a) Isomorphisms and commuting squares.
(c) Sublevel sets of $-f$ are superlevel sets of $f$.
(e) One of them contains the diagonal.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(e) Differences to sort: whether the diagonal is included; whether points carry
decorations; whether the index set is finite; whether the object is attached to
a function or to a module; whether infinite coordinates are allowed. ⟨your step:
go through the list and classify each, then say which single difference would
matter most for defining a distance.⟩
</details>

## Problem 4 (medium-hard — extended persistence, and why everything gets paired)

(a) Explain the problem elevation is meant to solve, and the two requirements
Edelsbrunner extracts from it: that the pairing agree for antipodal directions,
and that *all* critical points be paired. Say which of the two motivates
extending persistence.
(b) Write out the extended filtration: the ascending sequence of absolute
homology groups and the descending sequence of relative homology groups, with
the interleaved values $b_0 < a_1 < b_1 < \dots < a_n < b_n$. Then give the
one-line argument that everything born must die.
(c) State Rules 1, 2 and 3 relating classes of $\mathbb{M}^b$ to relative classes
of $(\mathbb{M}, \mathbb{M}^b)$, and define *essential* and *inessential*.
Edelsbrunner leaves the proof to the reader, "by studying the kernels and
cokernels"; set up the long exact sequence of the pair from at1-09 and say which
connecting map each rule is about.
(d) Work the genus-2 torus of Figure VII.9 in full. Reproduce the list of pairs
in each dimension, sort them into $\mathrm{Ord}$, $\mathrm{Ext}$ and
$\mathrm{Rel}$, and say which lie above the diagonal, which below, and which may
be either.
(e) State the Persistence Duality Theorem and the Persistence Symmetry Theorem.
Check the first against your answer to (d) with $d = 2$, and explain why for
$2p = d$ the extended sub-diagram is symmetric across the main diagonal.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(b) The sequence starts at 0 and ends at 0.
(d) There are two pairs in dimension 0, six in dimension 1, two in dimension 2.
(e) $\mathrm{Ext}_p(f) = \mathrm{Ext}_{d-p}^T(f)$; put $p = 1$, $d = 2$.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(d) Dimension 1: $(a_4, a_7), (a_5, a_6), (a_6, a_5), (a_7, a_4), (a_8, a_9),
(a_9, a_8)$. The class born at $a_8$ dies at $a_9$ on the way up, so that pair
is ordinary. ⟨your step: classify the other five, using which pass each birth
and each death happened on, and check that exactly four are extended.⟩
</details>

## Problem 5 (hard — "their space")

The unit is titled "Persistence diagrams and their space", and its mission strip
says: "Diagram space is the metric space (an2-01) where stability is stated;
lab-04 computed these, here they are defined."

(a) Search both sources for a metric on diagrams and report what you find.
Quote Edelsbrunner's deferral on p. 181 and locate Oudot's. Name the chapter in
each book where the metric actually appears.
(b) The one piece of structure in these pages that exists *for* a metric is the
diagonal, added to Edelsbrunner's diagrams with infinite multiplicity. Say what
job an infinite-multiplicity diagonal could do in a distance between two
multisets of different sizes, and construct the definition you would expect —
without claiming it is the source's.
(c) Take an2-01's axioms for a metric space and apply them as a checklist to
your construction in (b). For each axiom, say what would have to be proved.
Identify the one that is least obviously true for a distance defined by
optimising over matchings.
(d) Theorem 1.13 produces a *locally finite* multiset. Say what would go wrong
for a distance if local finiteness failed, using Oudot's own example of an
interval-decomposable module that is not $\mathfrak{q}$-tame.
(e) The audit, in three parts.

  First: the strip says "here they are defined", and that part is right — say
  precisely what this unit defines that tda1-04 did not, and for which class of
  modules.

  Second: the strip says diagram space "is the metric space where stability is
  stated". Neither resource states stability or defines that metric. Say what
  this unit is entitled to claim and write the corrected sentence.

  Third: the word *tame* has now appeared three times in this module in three
  unrelated senses — tame quivers (Oudot p. 17), $\mathfrak{q}$-tame modules
  (Definition 1.12), and tame functions (Edelsbrunner's duality and symmetry
  theorems). Oudot's own footnote on p. 32 relates two of them. Say which two,
  which way the implication runs, and which of the three the two duality
  theorems of Problem 4(e) need.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(b) What do you do with a point that has no partner?
(c) The triangle inequality.
(d) A dense set of points above the diagonal.
</details>
