## Problem 1 (easy — a deformation retraction, verified against the definition)

Let $X = \mathbb{R}^n \setminus \{0\}$ and let $A = S^{n-1}$ be the unit sphere.
Define, for $t \in I = [0,1]$,
$$f_t(x) \;=\; (1-t)\,x \;+\; t\,\frac{x}{|x|}.$$

(a) Check that $f_t(x) \in X$ for every $x \in X$ and every $t$ — that is, that
$f_t(x) \neq 0$. (This is the step most people skip.)
(b) Verify each of the three conditions in Hatcher's definition of a
deformation retraction of $X$ onto $A$: $f_0 = \mathbb{1}$, $f_1(X) = A$, and
$f_t|A = \mathbb{1}$ for all $t$.
(c) Verify the continuity condition — that the associated map
$X \times I \to X$, $(x,t) \mapsto f_t(x)$, is continuous. Say where you use
that $0$ has been removed.
(d) Does the same formula define a deformation retraction of $\mathbb{R}^n$
onto $S^{n-1}$? Answer with a reason, not just yes or no.

*(Hatcher, ch. 0, p. 2)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
For (a), $x$ and $x/|x|$ point the same way. What does that tell you about a
positive combination of them? For (d), look at what your answer to (a) needed.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
Write $f_t(x) = \lambda(t,x)\,x$ for a scalar $\lambda$ and read off everything
from $\lambda$. Then (a) becomes "$\lambda > 0$", (b) becomes three evaluations
of $\lambda$, and (c) reduces to continuity of $\lambda$, since scalar
multiplication $\mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ is continuous.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
$\lambda(t,x) = (1-t) + t/|x|$, which is strictly positive for $t \in [0,1]$ and
$x \neq 0$. On $A$ we have $|x| = 1$, so $\lambda \equiv 1$. The map
$(x,t) \mapsto |x|$ is continuous on $\mathbb{R}^n$ and non-zero on $X$.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) Since $f_t(x) = \big((1-t) + t/|x|\big)x$ and both $(1-t) \geq 0$ and
$t/|x| \geq 0$ with at least one strictly positive for every $t \in [0,1]$, the
scalar $\lambda(t,x) = (1-t) + t/|x|$ is strictly positive. As $x \neq 0$,
$f_t(x) = \lambda x \neq 0$, so $f_t(x) \in X$.

(b) $f_0(x) = x$, so $f_0 = \mathbb{1}$. For $f_1$: $f_1(x) = x/|x|$, which has
norm $1$, so $f_1(X) \subseteq A$; and $f_1|A = \mathbb{1}$ gives $f_1(X) = A$.
For $x \in A$, $|x| = 1$ so $\lambda(t,x) = (1-t) + t = 1$ and $f_t(x) = x$ for
every $t$ — this is $f_t|A = \mathbb{1}$.

(c) ⟨your step: name the continuous maps you are composing, and say why
$|x| \neq 0$ matters⟩

(d) ⟨your step⟩
</details>

## Problem 2 (easy–medium — retraction, and what a deformation retraction adds)

Hatcher records two descriptions of a retraction of $X$ onto $A$: a map
$r : X \to X$ with $r(X) = A$ and $r|A = \mathbb{1}$; and, "from a more formal
viewpoint", a map $r : X \to X$ with $r^2 = r$.

(a) Prove the two agree in the following sense: if $r : X \to X$ satisfies
$r(X) = A$ and $r|A = \mathbb{1}$ then $r^2 = r$; and conversely, if $r^2 = r$
then $r$ is a retraction onto its image.
(b) Show that every non-empty space $X$ retracts onto each of its points.
(c) Prove Hatcher's observation that a space which deformation retracts onto a
point $x_0$ must be path-connected.
(d) Conclude that $X = \{0,1\}$ with the discrete topology retracts onto
$\{0\}$ but does not deformation retract onto $\{0\}$. Which clause of the
definition of deformation retraction fails, and at which $t$?

*(Hatcher, ch. 0, p. 3)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
For (c) you already have the path: a deformation retraction is a *family*
$f_t$, and fixing $x$ and letting $t$ run is a map $I \to X$.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
(a) is two one-line arguments; the content is that $r^2 = r$ says exactly "$r$
is the identity on its image". For (c), define $\gamma(t) = f_t(x)$ and check
continuity by restricting the continuous map $X \times I \to X$ to the slice
$\{x\} \times I$ — this is where top-04 does the work. For (d), do not look for
a discontinuity in a formula; ask what $f_t(1)$ can possibly be.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(c) $\gamma(0) = x$ and $\gamma(1) = x_0$, so every point is joined to $x_0$;
concatenating two such paths joins any two points. (d) $\{0,1\}$ discrete is
not path-connected.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) Suppose $r(X) = A$ and $r|A = \mathbb{1}$. For any $x \in X$ we have
$r(x) \in A$, so $r(r(x)) = r(x)$ because $r$ is the identity on $A$. Hence
$r^2 = r$. Conversely suppose $r^2 = r$ and put $A = r(X)$. Any $a \in A$ is
$a = r(x)$ for some $x$, so $r(a) = r(r(x)) = r(x) = a$; thus $r|A = \mathbb{1}$,
and $r(X) = A$ by definition of $A$.

(b) The constant map $c_{x_0}(x) = x_0$ is continuous, has image $\{x_0\}$, and
restricts to the identity there.

(c) Let $f_t$ be a deformation retraction of $X$ onto $\{x_0\}$ and fix
$x \in X$. Define $\gamma : I \to X$ by $\gamma(t) = f_t(x)$.
⟨your step: why is $\gamma$ continuous, and what are $\gamma(0)$, $\gamma(1)$?⟩

(d) ⟨your step⟩
</details>

## Problem 3 (medium — homotopy equivalence is an equivalence relation)

Hatcher calls this "an easy exercise", and contrasts it with the
"nonsymmetric notion of deformation retraction". Do the exercise carefully; the
transitivity step is the one with content.

(a) Show $\simeq$ is reflexive on spaces: $X \simeq X$.
(b) Show it is symmetric, directly from the definition of homotopy equivalence.
(c) Show it is transitive. You will need the fact that homotopy of maps is
compatible with composition: if $f_0 \simeq f_1 : X \to Y$ and $g$ is a map
$Y \to Z$, then $g f_0 \simeq g f_1$; and if $h : W \to X$ then
$f_0 h \simeq f_1 h$. Prove both compatibilities before using them.
(d) Explain in one sentence why the corresponding statement for "is a
deformation retract of" fails, using Hatcher's three graphs — the circle with
two whiskers, the figure eight, and the theta graph — which are pairwise
homotopy equivalent while none is a deformation retract of any other.

*(Hatcher, ch. 0, p. 3)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
For (c), if $f : X \to Y$ and $f' : Y \to Z$ have homotopy inverses $g$ and
$g'$, the only candidate inverse for $f'f$ is $gg'$. Push the homotopies through
one composition at a time.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
For the compatibility in (c): if $F : X \times I \to Y$ is a homotopy from $f_0$
to $f_1$, then $g \circ F$ is a homotopy from $gf_0$ to $gf_1$, and
$F \circ (h \times \mathbb{1}_I)$ is one from $f_0h$ to $f_1h$. Both are
continuous as composites of continuous maps (top-04). Then chain:
$(gg')(f'f) = g(g'f')f \simeq g \mathbb{1} f = gf \simeq \mathbb{1}$.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(a) Take $g = \mathbb{1}_X$. (b) The definition is symmetric in $f$ and $g$:
if $f$ is a homotopy equivalence with inverse $g$, then $g$ is one with inverse
$f$. (d) A deformation retract is a *subspace*; the relation cannot be
symmetric between spaces that are not subspaces of each other.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(c) Compatibility, first half. Let $F : X \times I \to Y$ be continuous with
$F(\cdot,0) = f_0$ and $F(\cdot,1) = f_1$, and let $g : Y \to Z$. Then
$g \circ F : X \times I \to Z$ is continuous, and
$(g \circ F)(\cdot,0) = gf_0$, $(g \circ F)(\cdot,1) = gf_1$. So
$gf_0 \simeq gf_1$.

Compatibility, second half. ⟨your step⟩

Transitivity. Let $f : X \to Y$ with inverse $g$, and $f' : Y \to Z$ with
inverse $g'$. Then
$$(gg')(f'f) = g\,(g'f')\,f \simeq g\,\mathbb{1}_Y\,f = gf \simeq \mathbb{1}_X,$$
where the first $\simeq$ uses both compatibilities applied to $g'f' \simeq \mathbb{1}_Y$.
⟨your step: the other composite⟩
</details>

## Problem 4 (medium–hard — the mapping cylinder)

For a map $f : X \to Y$, the mapping cylinder $M_f$ is the quotient space of
the disjoint union $(X \times I) \sqcup Y$ obtained by identifying each
$(x,1) \in X \times I$ with $f(x) \in Y$. (top-13: this is a quotient of a
disjoint union, so a map out of $M_f$ is continuous exactly when its two
restrictions are.)

(a) Describe $M_f$ concretely when $X = S^1$, $Y$ is a point, and $f$ is the
only map there is. Then describe it when $X = S^1$, $Y = S^1$ and
$f = \mathbb{1}$.
(b) Prove that $M_f$ deformation retracts onto the subspace $Y$, by sliding
each point $(x,s)$ along the segment $\{x\} \times I$ to the endpoint
$f(x) \in Y$. Write down the formula for $f_t$ and check the three conditions.
State clearly where you use the universal property of the quotient.
(c) Deduce that the inclusion $Y \hookrightarrow M_f$ is a homotopy
equivalence, using Hatcher's observation that a deformation retraction of $X$
onto $A$ gives $ri = \mathbb{1}$ and $ir \simeq \mathbb{1}$.
(d) Corollary 0.21 states that $f : X \to Y$ is a homotopy equivalence if and
only if $X$ is a deformation retract of $M_f$, and hence that two spaces are
homotopy equivalent if and only if some third space contains both as
deformation retracts. Using only part (b) and Corollary 0.21, explain which of
the two implications in Corollary 0.21 is the one part (b) does *not* give you,
and why the other end of the cylinder is the hard end.

*(Hatcher, ch. 0, p. 2 for the cylinder and its retraction onto $Y$; p. 16 for
Corollary 0.21)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
In (b), the formula must not move points of $Y$, and at $t=1$ everything must
land in $Y$. Try $f_t(x,s) = (x, s + t(1-s))$ on the cylinder part.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
Define the homotopy first on $(X \times I) \sqcup Y$ — on $Y$ it is constant,
on $X \times I$ it pushes $s$ towards $1$ — then check it respects the
identification $(x,1) \sim f(x)$, which is exactly what lets it descend to
$M_f$. For (d): part (b) shows one end always deformation retracts, with no
hypothesis on $f$ at all. Corollary 0.21 is about the *other* end, and there
the hypothesis that $f$ is a homotopy equivalence is doing real work.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(a) $S^1$ with $Y$ a point gives the cone on $S^1$, a disk. With $f = \mathbb{1}$
it gives the cylinder $S^1 \times I$. (b) At $t = 1$ the map sends $(x,s)$ to
$(x,1) \sim f(x) \in Y$, so the image is $Y$.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(b) Define $\tilde{f}_t$ on the disjoint union by
$$\tilde{f}_t(x,s) = (x,\; s + t(1-s)) \quad \text{on } X \times I,
\qquad \tilde{f}_t(y) = y \quad \text{on } Y.$$
Each is continuous in $(\text{point}, t)$ jointly, so $\tilde{f}$ is continuous
on $\big((X\times I) \sqcup Y\big) \times I$.

Respecting the identification: a point $(x,1)$ has
$\tilde f_t(x,1) = (x, 1 + t(1-1)) = (x,1)$, which is identified with $f(x)$;
and the copy of $f(x)$ in $Y$ has $\tilde f_t(f(x)) = f(x)$. The two agree, so
$\tilde f_t$ descends to a well-defined $f_t : M_f \to M_f$, continuous by the
universal property of the quotient.

Now the three conditions: $f_0(x,s) = (x,s)$ and $f_0(y) = y$, so
$f_0 = \mathbb{1}$. ⟨your step: $f_1(M_f) = Y$⟩ ⟨your step: $f_t|Y = \mathbb{1}$⟩

(c) ⟨your step⟩ (d) ⟨your step⟩
</details>

## Problem 5 (hard — contractibility, and collapsing)

A space is *contractible* if it has the homotopy type of a point, equivalently
if its identity map is *nullhomotopic* — homotopic to a constant map.

(a) Prove the equivalence just asserted: $X$ is homotopy equivalent to a
one-point space if and only if $\mathbb{1}_X$ is nullhomotopic.
(b) Let $X$ be contractible. Prove that any two maps $g_0, g_1 : W \to X$ are
homotopic, for any space $W$. Deduce that a contractible space is
path-connected.
(c) Hatcher notes that contractibility is "slightly weaker" than deformation
retracting to a point. Using your answer to (a), identify precisely which
clause of the definition of deformation retraction is *not* implied by
$\mathbb{1}_X \simeq \text{const}$. (You are not asked to construct a space
distinguishing them — Hatcher defers that to the chapter's exercises.)
(d) Proposition 0.17 states that if $(X,A)$ satisfies the homotopy extension
property and $A$ is contractible, then the quotient map $q : X \to X/A$ is a
homotopy equivalence. Corollary 0.20 states that if $(X,A)$ satisfies the
homotopy extension property and the inclusion $A \hookrightarrow X$ is a
homotopy equivalence, then $A$ is a deformation retract of $X$.

Both are stated with the homotopy extension property as a hypothesis. Give the
one-sentence reason, drawn from your answer to (c), why neither can drop it —
that is, say what the homotopy extension property supplies that a bare
homotopy equivalence does not. Then state what Corollary 0.20 gives you when
combined with Problem 4(c).

*(Hatcher, ch. 0, p. 4 for contractibility; p. 15 for Proposition 0.17 and for
Example 0.15; p. 16 for Corollary 0.20; the mapping cylinder neighbourhood
$X \times [0,1/2]$ and the $f = ri$, $i \simeq jf$ diagram are lifted from
Hatcher's own proof of Corollary 0.21, p. 17)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
In (c), compare "there is a homotopy from $\mathbb{1}_X$ to the constant map at
$x_0$" with "there is such a homotopy that additionally fixes $x_0$ at every
time $t$".
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
(a) One direction: compose the homotopy equivalence with its inverse. The other:
a nullhomotopy $\mathbb{1}_X \simeq c_{x_0}$ *is* the composite condition, with
the two maps $X \to \{x_0\}$ and $\{x_0\} \to X$. (b) Push a nullhomotopy of
$\mathbb{1}_X$ along $g_i$ using the composition compatibility from Problem 3.
(d) A deformation retraction is a homotopy *rel $A$*; the homotopy extension
property is exactly the tool that upgrades a homotopy defined on a subspace to
one on the whole space, which is what lets you impose the rel condition.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
(c) The failing clause is $f_t|A = \mathbb{1}$ for all $t$ — with
$A = \{x_0\}$, the nullhomotopy need not keep $x_0$ fixed while it runs.
(d) Combined with Problem 4(c): $Y$ is a deformation retract of $M_f$ for
*every* $f$ — but that is not what Corollary 0.20 consumes, and on its own it
gives you nothing here. It says only that $Y \hookrightarrow M_f$ is a homotopy
equivalence, whereas applying the corollary to the pair $(M_f, X)$ needs both
of its hypotheses checked for $X$. One of them is a citation: $(M_f, X)$ has
the homotopy extension property by Example 0.15, taking $X \times [0, 1/2]$ as
the mapping cylinder neighbourhood of $X$ in $M_f$. The other is the work.
⟨your step: show that $X \hookrightarrow M_f$ is a homotopy equivalence when
$f$ is. Write $i : X \hookrightarrow M_f$ and $j : Y \hookrightarrow M_f$ for
the inclusions and $r : M_f \to Y$ for the canonical retraction; check that
$f = ri$ and $i \simeq jf$; then use that $j$ and $r$ are homotopy
equivalences.⟩ With both hypotheses in hand Corollary 0.20 gives $X$ as a
deformation retract of $M_f$ — which is the forward half of Corollary 0.21.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) ($\Leftarrow$) Suppose $H : X \times I \to X$ is a homotopy with
$H(\cdot,0) = \mathbb{1}_X$ and $H(\cdot,1) = c_{x_0}$. Let $P = \{x_0\}$, let
$i : P \to X$ be the inclusion and $r : X \to P$ the only map. Then
$ri = \mathbb{1}_P$ trivially, and $ir = c_{x_0} \simeq \mathbb{1}_X$ via $H$.
So $i$ is a homotopy equivalence and $X \simeq P$.

($\Rightarrow$) ⟨your step⟩

(b) Let $H$ be a nullhomotopy of $\mathbb{1}_X$ as above and let
$g : W \to X$ be any map. By the composition compatibility of Problem 3(c),
$g = \mathbb{1}_X \circ g \simeq c_{x_0} \circ g = c_{x_0}$. So every map into
$X$ is homotopic to the same constant map $c_{x_0}$; hence any two are
homotopic to each other. ⟨your step: now deduce path-connectedness by taking
$W$ to be a point⟩

(c) ⟨your step⟩ (d) ⟨your step⟩
</details>
