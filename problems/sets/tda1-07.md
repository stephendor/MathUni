# tda1-07 — The stability theorem

**Module:** TDA I · **Unit:** tda1-07
**Sources:** Oudot, *Persistence Theory: From Quiver Representations to Data
Analysis* — Chapter 3 "Stability", §2, §3 and §4: Lesnick's lifting of the
interleaving distance back to the topological level, with its restrictions

observation that the discretization grid may be shifted (pp. 54–55); the proof

interleaving of (3.8) (pp. 55–57); §2.2's Lemma 3.5 (Interpolation) with the
shifted diagonals $\Delta_r$, the correspondence (3.9), and Figure 3.5's three
choices of extension — cokernels, images, kernels (pp. 57–60); §3's converse
stability proof in two steps, the $\varepsilon$-smoothing $\mathbb{V}^\varepsilon$
with $V_i^\varepsilon = \operatorname{im} v_{i-\varepsilon}^{i+\varepsilon}$,
the density of $\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{pfd}}$ in $\mathcal{P}_\mathfrak{q}$, and

§4's history, Corollary 3.6 with footnote 4, the account of what the original
Cohen-Steiner–Edelsbrunner–Harer hypotheses cost in applications, Theorem 3.7,
and Theorem 3.8 with the warning that its bound depends on total persistences,
"which are unstable quantities" (pp. 61–63).
Edelsbrunner and Harer, *Computational Topology: An Introduction* — §VIII.2's
Stability Theorem for Filtrations and Stability Theorem for Tame Functions, the
vineyard slope bound that proves the first, and the definition of a tame
function (pp. 216–219).

**The scope warning this unit is under.** The strip calls stability "why
persistence is trustworthy on noisy data". The theorem bounds a bottleneck
distance by a supremum norm. Problem 5 works out exactly which chain of
inferences that licenses and which it does not, using Oudot's own account of
what the original hypotheses cost "especially in the presence of noise in the
data" (p. 62).

Interleaves tda1-06, which defined both metrics and stated Theorem 3.1;
tda1-05, whose persistence measure the Snapping Lemma computes with; an2-05;
and pw-04.

## Problem 2 (medium — snapping)

(a) Define the $\varepsilon$-discretization $\mathbb{V}_{\varepsilon\mathbb{Z}}$
and the convention for extending it back to a module over $\mathbb{R}$.
(b) Explain the snapping principle on interval summands: show that a summand
$\mathbb{I}\lceil b^\pm, d^\pm \rfloor$ with $(n-1)\varepsilon < b < n\varepsilon$
and $(m-1)\varepsilon < d < m\varepsilon$ becomes $\mathbb{I}[n\varepsilon,
m\varepsilon)$, and say why births and deaths both round *up*.
(c) Say what happens in the limit cases $b = n\varepsilon$ or $d = m\varepsilon$,

(e) Lemma 3.4 ends by asserting a partial matching of bottleneck cost at most
$\varepsilon$. Verify the bound: given the snapping rule, what is the largest

## Problem 5 (hard — what stability licenses)

The mission strip says: "The stability theorem is why persistence is trustworthy
on noisy data — the mission's keystone; the isometry theorem (bottleneck =
interleaving distance) is its algebraic partner."

(a) **The second clause first, because it is a category error.** Oudot writes
that "the statement of the theorem falls into two parts", stability and converse
stability. Say what relation the strip asserts between stability and the isometry
theorem, what the relation actually is, and rewrite the clause.
(b) **What the theorem quantifies over.** Write Corollary 3.6 out with all its
hypotheses. Then answer precisely: if a measurement process produces $g$ rather
than $f$, what must be true of the relation between $f$ and $g$ for the corollary
to apply at all? Give one realistic way of corrupting data that does *not*
satisfy it.
(c) **What the conclusion controls.** The conclusion bounds
$\mathrm{d_b}(\mathrm{dgm}(f), \mathrm{dgm}(g))$. Suppose a downstream procedure
reports a number computed from a diagram — a total persistence, a maximum bar
length, a count of bars above a threshold. For each of the three, say whether a
bound on $\mathrm{d_b}$ bounds the change in that number, and prove or refute it.
(d) Oudot's Theorem 3.8 bounds $W_p$ in terms of $\mathrm{d_i}$ and the total
persistences, and he immediately warns that these "are unstable quantities". Use
your answer to (c) to say why that warning was predictable, and what it implies
about chaining stability results.
(e) The audit. Oudot writes, of the original hypotheses, that requiring finite
triangulability and continuity "does have an impact on applications, where it is
common to deal with spaces or maps that do not comply with the requirements,
especially in the presence of noise in the data". Write the corrected mission
strip. It should say what stability does establish, in the metric it establishes
it in, and should not assert anything about a chain from a data perturbation to
a reported statistic that these pages do not close.

<details><summary>Hint</summary>
(a) A part is not a partner.
(b) The corollary is about $\|f-g\|_\infty$.
(c) One of the three is fine; one is badly behaved; one depends on the
threshold.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>

(c) A bound of $\varepsilon$ on $\mathrm{d_b}$ says every point moves by at most

<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
(c) is where the unit earns its place. Stability is a theorem about a distance
between diagrams, and almost nothing anyone reports is a distance between
diagrams. The question is always which functionals of the diagram are Lipschitz
with respect to $\mathrm{d_b}$ — and the answer is: fewer than one would like,
which is exactly why Theorem 3.8's bound has an unstable quantity in it. Do the
counterexample in (c) by hand before reading (d); it makes (d) obvious.
</details>
