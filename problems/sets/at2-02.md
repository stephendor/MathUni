## Problem 1 (easy — computing Ext and Hom) [interleaves aa-20]

Using only the three properties of Ext stated in §3.1 —
$\operatorname{Ext}(H \oplus H', G) \cong \operatorname{Ext}(H,G) \oplus \operatorname{Ext}(H',G)$,
$\operatorname{Ext}(H,G) = 0$ for $H$ free, and
$\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_n, G) \cong G/nG$ — compute:

(a) $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z})$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}, \mathbb{Z})$;
(b) $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_6, \mathbb{Z})$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_6, \mathbb{Z})$;
(c) $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_4, \mathbb{Z})$ and $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_4, \mathbb{Z})$;
(d) $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_4, \mathbb{Z}_6)$.

Then state, in one sentence each, the two general facts §3.1 records for
finitely generated $H$: what $\operatorname{Ext}(H,\mathbb{Z})$ is, and what
$\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathbb{Z})$ is.

*(Hatcher §3.1, pp. 195–196)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
$G/nG$ means the quotient of $G$ by the subgroup $\{ng : g \in G\}$. For
$G = \mathbb{Z}$ that subgroup is $n\mathbb{Z}$.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
Every finitely generated abelian group is a direct sum of a free part and cyclic
torsion summands (aa-20), so the first property reduces everything to the second
and third. For (d) work out $4\mathbb{Z}_6$ as a subset before taking the
quotient.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
$\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_6,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}_6$,
while $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_6,\mathbb{Z}) = 0$.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) $\mathbb{Z}$ is free, so $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}) = 0$; and
$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}$, a homomorphism out
of $\mathbb{Z}$ being determined by the image of $1$.
(b) $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_6,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}_6$.
$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_6,\mathbb{Z}) = 0$: the image of the generator has
order dividing $6$, and $\mathbb{Z}$ is torsion-free, so it must be $0$.
(c) By the first property, $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_4, \mathbb{Z}) \cong 0 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_4 = \mathbb{Z}_4$ and
$\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_4, \mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus 0 = \mathbb{Z}$.
(d) $4\mathbb{Z}_6 = \{0,4,8 \bmod 6\} = \{0,4,2\} = 2\mathbb{Z}_6$, a subgroup of
order $3$, so $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_4,\mathbb{Z}_6) \cong \mathbb{Z}_6/2\mathbb{Z}_6 \cong \mathbb{Z}_2$.
Note this is neither $\mathbb{Z}_4$ nor $\mathbb{Z}_6$ — Ext is not simply
"the torsion" once the coefficient group has torsion of its own.
The two general facts: for finitely generated $H$,
$\operatorname{Ext}(H,\mathbb{Z})$ is isomorphic to the torsion subgroup of $H$,
and $\operatorname{Hom}(H,\mathbb{Z})$ is isomorphic to the free part of $H$.
</details>

## Problem 2 (easy — reading Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.3)

Let $C$ be a chain complex of free abelian groups with
$H_0(C) = \mathbb{Z}$, $H_1(C) = \mathbb{Z}_2$, $H_2(C) = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_3$,
and $H_n(C) = 0$ for $n \geq 3$.

(a) Write out the split short exact sequence of Theorem 3.2 for $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$
with $G = \mathbb{Z}$, naming both outer terms explicitly.
(b) Use Corollary 3.3 to compute $H^n(C;\mathbb{Z})$ for $n = 0,1,2,3$.
(c) Check that your answers to (a) and (b) agree, and say which dimension the
$\mathbb{Z}_2$ appears in and which dimension the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ appears in.
(d) Now take $G = \mathbb{Q}$. Compute $H^n(C;\mathbb{Q})$ for all $n$ and say
what happened to the torsion.

*(Hatcher §3.1, Theorem 3.2 and Corollary 3.3, pp. 195–196)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
Corollary 3.3 reads $H^n(C;\mathbb{Z}) \cong (H_n/T_n) \oplus T_{n-1}$ — index
$n$ on the free part, index $n-1$ on the torsion.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
For (d), note $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_m,\mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Q}/m\mathbb{Q}$
and multiplication by $m$ is invertible on $\mathbb{Q}$.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
$H^0 = \mathbb{Z}$, $H^1 = 0$, $H^2 = \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2$,
$H^3 = \mathbb{Z}_3$.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) For each $n$, Theorem 3.2 gives
$0 \to \operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1}(C),\mathbb{Z}) \to H^n(C;\mathbb{Z}) \to \operatorname{Hom}(H_n(C),\mathbb{Z}) \to 0$.
$n=0$: $0 \to 0 \to H^0 \to \mathbb{Z} \to 0$ (there is no $H_{-1}$).
$n=1$: $0 \to \operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z}) = 0 \to H^1 \to \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_2,\mathbb{Z}) = 0 \to 0$.
$n=2$: $0 \to \operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_2,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}_2 \to H^2 \to \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_3,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z} \to 0$.
$n=3$: $0 \to \operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_3,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}_3 \to H^3 \to \operatorname{Hom}(0,\mathbb{Z}) = 0 \to 0$.
(b) $T_0 = 0$, $T_1 = \mathbb{Z}_2$, $T_2 = \mathbb{Z}_3$. So
$H^0 \cong \mathbb{Z}$, $H^1 \cong (\mathbb{Z}_2/\mathbb{Z}_2) \oplus 0 = 0$,
$H^2 \cong \mathbb{Z} \oplus \mathbb{Z}_2$, $H^3 \cong 0 \oplus \mathbb{Z}_3 = \mathbb{Z}_3$.
(c) They agree, the sequences being split. The $\mathbb{Z}_2$ from $H_1$ appears
in $H^2$ and the $\mathbb{Z}_3$ from $H_2$ appears in $H^3$: every torsion
summand moves up exactly one dimension.
(d) Over $\mathbb{Q}$, $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_m,\mathbb{Q}) = \mathbb{Q}/m\mathbb{Q} = 0$
since $m$ is invertible, and $\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z}_m,\mathbb{Q}) = 0$.
So $H^0 = \mathbb{Q}$, $H^1 = 0$, $H^2 = \mathbb{Q}$, $H^3 = 0$: the torsion has
vanished entirely, leaving only the ranks.
</details>

## Problem 3 (medium — free resolutions and where $G/nG$ comes from)

(a) Give the standard two-term free resolution
$0 \to F_1 \to F_0 \to H \to 0$ of an arbitrary abelian group $H$, explaining why
$F_1$ may be taken free, and say why it follows that $H^n(F;G) = 0$ for $n > 1$
for *every* free resolution of $H$, not just this one.
(b) Write out the free resolution
$0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}_n \to 0$, dualise it
by $\operatorname{Hom}(-,G)$, and derive $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_n,G) \cong G/nG$
from the resulting complex. State exactly which group is the kernel and which is
the image.
(c) Use (b) with $n = 4$ and $G = \mathbb{Z}$ to compute $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_4,\mathbb{Z})$,
and check your answer against Problem 1.
(d) Explain why $\operatorname{Ext}_R(H,G) = 0$ whenever $R$ is a field and $H$,
$G$ are $R$-modules. Name the consequence for persistence computed over
$\mathbf{k} = \mathbb{F}_p$.

*(Hatcher §3.1, pp. 194–196; the $R$-module version, p. 196)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
The kernel of a surjection onto $H$ from a free abelian group is a subgroup of a
free abelian group, hence free.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
For (a)'s second half, quote Lemma 3.1(b): $H^n(F;G)$ does not depend on the
resolution, so a single resolution with $F_n = 0$ for $n > 1$ settles it for all.
For (d), over a field every module is free, so one may take the resolution
$0 \to F_0 \to H \to 0$.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
Dualising gives $0 \to \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},G) \xrightarrow{n} \operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},G) \to 0$,
i.e. $0 \to G \xrightarrow{n} G \to 0$, so $\operatorname{Ext} = G/nG$ at the
right-hand spot.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) Choose generators for $H$, let $F_0$ be free on a basis matching them, and
let $f_0 : F_0 \to H$ send basis elements to generators; $f_0$ is surjective.
Set $F_1 = \operatorname{Ker} f_0$ with $f_1$ the inclusion. $F_1$ is a subgroup
of the free abelian group $F_0$, hence free, and we may take $F_i = 0$ for
$i > 1$. For this resolution $H^n(F;G) = 0$ when $n > 1$ because the complex is
zero there; and by Lemma 3.1(b) the groups $H^n(F;G)$ are independent of the
chosen resolution, so this holds for every free resolution. Hence the only
interesting group is $H^1(F;G)$, and that is what is called
$\operatorname{Ext}(H,G)$.
(b) Dualising $0 \to \mathbb{Z} \xrightarrow{n} \mathbb{Z} \to \mathbb{Z}_n \to 0$
by $\operatorname{Hom}(-,G)$, and deleting the $H^*$ term as the definition of
$H^n(F;G)$ requires, leaves $G \xrightarrow{n} G$ in degrees $0$ and $1$. So
$\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_n,G) = H^1(F;G) = G/\operatorname{Im}(n) = G/nG$,
with $\operatorname{Im}$ the subgroup $nG$ and no further kernel condition, the
complex being zero in degree $2$.
(c) $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_4,\mathbb{Z}) = \mathbb{Z}/4\mathbb{Z} = \mathbb{Z}_4$,
matching Problem 1(c).
(d) Over a field $R$ every module is free, so $H$ admits the free resolution
$0 \to F_0 \to H \to 0$ with $F_1 = 0$; hence $\operatorname{Ext}_R(H,G) = H^1(F;G) = 0$.
For persistence over $\mathbf{k} = \mathbb{F}_p$ this means the universal
coefficient sequence collapses to an isomorphism $H^n \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_n,\mathbf{k})$:
cohomology is the plain dual of homology, the ranks agree, and the barcode is
the same whichever side you compute — which is what at2-08 exploits.
</details>

## Problem 4 (medium — the extension part of Lemma 3.1)

Let $F$ and $F'$ be free resolutions of abelian groups $H$ and $H'$, and let
$\alpha : H \to H'$ be a homomorphism.

(a) Construct $\alpha_0 : F_0 \to F_0'$ making the first square commute, saying
exactly where surjectivity of $f_0'$ and freeness of $F_0$ are used.
(b) Carry out the inductive step: assuming $\alpha_{i-1}$ and $\alpha_{i-2}$ are
built, construct $\alpha_i$, and verify the element you need lies in
$\operatorname{Im} f_i'$.
(c) State the second half of Lemma 3.1(a) and explain, without reproducing the
whole proof, why *some* statement of that kind is needed before
$\operatorname{Ext}(H,G)$ can be called well defined.
(d) Lemma 3.1(b) concludes that $H^n(F;G) \cong H^n(F';G)$ canonically. Which
part of (a) supplies the two mutually inverse maps?

*(Hatcher §3.1, Lemma 3.1, p. 194)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
Freeness lets you define a homomorphism by choosing images of basis elements
arbitrarily; surjectivity guarantees a suitable image exists to choose.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
For (b), you want $x'$ with $f_i'(x') = \alpha_{i-1} f_i(x)$; such an $x'$ exists
provided $\alpha_{i-1} f_i(x) \in \operatorname{Im} f_i' = \operatorname{Ker} f_{i-1}'$,
so compute $f_{i-1}' \alpha_{i-1} f_i(x)$ and use the previous square plus
$f_{i-1} f_i = 0$.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
For $i = 1$: $f_0' \alpha_0 f_1 = \alpha f_0 f_1 = 0$, so $\alpha_0 f_1(x)$ lies
in $\operatorname{Ker} f_0' = \operatorname{Im} f_1'$.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) $F_0$ is free, so it suffices to define $\alpha_0$ on a basis. For a basis
element $x \in F_0$ the element $\alpha f_0(x)$ lies in $H'$, and $f_0'$ is
surjective, so there is $x' \in F_0'$ with $f_0'(x') = \alpha f_0(x)$; set
$\alpha_0(x) = x'$. Commutativity $f_0' \alpha_0 = \alpha f_0$ holds on the
basis, hence everywhere.
(b) Suppose $\alpha_{i-1}$ is constructed. For a basis element $x \in F_i$ we
want $x' \in F_i'$ with $f_i'(x') = \alpha_{i-1} f_i(x)$. Such an $x'$ exists iff
$\alpha_{i-1} f_i(x) \in \operatorname{Im} f_i' = \operatorname{Ker} f_{i-1}'$,
and indeed $f_{i-1}' \alpha_{i-1} f_i = \alpha_{i-2} f_{i-1} f_i = 0$ by the
previous square and exactness of $F$. Define $\alpha_i(x) = x'$ and extend by
freeness.
(c) The second half says that any two chain maps extending $\alpha$ are chain
homotopic. It is needed because $\alpha_0$ and the $\alpha_i$ involved
*choices* of preimages; without knowing that different choices are chain
homotopic, the induced maps on the dualised complexes — and hence the
identification of $\operatorname{Ext}$ computed from different resolutions —
would depend on those choices, and $\operatorname{Ext}(H,G)$ would not be a
well-defined group attached to $H$ and $G$.
(d) Take $\alpha$ to be the identity of $H$ but with two different resolutions
$F$ and $F'$: part (a) produces a chain map each way, and the composites are
chain homotopic to the identity, so the induced maps on cohomology are mutually
inverse isomorphisms.
</details>

## Problem 5 (hard — proving Corollary 3.3, and the limits of the theorem) [interleaves aa-20]

(a) Prove Corollary 3.3: if $H_n$ and $H_{n-1}$ are finitely generated with
torsion subgroups $T_n \subseteq H_n$ and $T_{n-1} \subseteq H_{n-1}$, then
$H^n(C;\mathbb{Z}) \cong (H_n/T_n) \oplus T_{n-1}$. Use Theorem 3.2, the
structure theorem for finitely generated abelian groups (aa-20), and the two
general facts you stated in Problem 1.
(b) Hatcher notes that the splitting in Theorem 3.2 is *natural in neither*
sense: the short exact sequences are natural, but the splitting is not, because
it depends on a choice. Identify the choice, and explain why naturality of the
sequences is nonetheless enough to prove Corollary 3.4.
(c) State Corollary 3.4 and prove it from naturality plus the five lemma,
naming the five maps you feed to the five lemma.
(d) Give a chain complex of free abelian groups and a coefficient group $G$ for
which $H^n(C;G) \not\cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_n(C),G)$, and one for which the
two *are* isomorphic for every $n$ although $C$ has torsion in its homology.
Explain what distinguishes the two cases.

*(Hatcher §3.1, Theorem 3.2, Corollary 3.3, Corollary 3.4, pp. 195–196)*

<details><summary>Nudge</summary>
For (a), split $H_n \cong (H_n/T_n) \oplus T_n$ and apply Hom and Ext summand by
summand.
</details>
<details><summary>Strategy</summary>
(b) The splitting comes from the projection $p : C_n \to Z_n$ chosen in at2-01,
and a chain map need not respect a choice of $p$ on both complexes. (d) The
second case wants a coefficient group in which the relevant multiplications are
invertible.
</details>
<details><summary>Partial</summary>
$\operatorname{Hom}(H_n,\mathbb{Z}) \cong H_n/T_n$ and
$\operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1},\mathbb{Z}) \cong T_{n-1}$; the sequence splits;
combine.
</details>
<details><summary>Worked start</summary>
(a) By Theorem 3.2 there is a split short exact sequence
$0 \to \operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1},\mathbb{Z}) \to H^n(C;\mathbb{Z}) \to \operatorname{Hom}(H_n,\mathbb{Z}) \to 0$,
so $H^n(C;\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1},\mathbb{Z}) \oplus \operatorname{Hom}(H_n,\mathbb{Z})$.
By the structure theorem, $H_n \cong (H_n/T_n) \oplus T_n$ with $H_n/T_n$ free of
finite rank and $T_n$ a finite direct sum of cyclic groups. Hence
$\operatorname{Hom}(H_n,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Hom}(H_n/T_n,\mathbb{Z}) \oplus \operatorname{Hom}(T_n,\mathbb{Z}) \cong (H_n/T_n) \oplus 0$,
and $\operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1},\mathbb{Z}) \cong \operatorname{Ext}(H_{n-1}/T_{n-1},\mathbb{Z}) \oplus \operatorname{Ext}(T_{n-1},\mathbb{Z}) \cong 0 \oplus T_{n-1}$,
using $\operatorname{Ext}(\mathbb{Z}_m,\mathbb{Z}) \cong \mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z}$
on each cyclic summand. Substituting gives the claim.
(b) The choice is the projection $p : C_n \to Z_n$ used to extend homomorphisms
$Z_n \to G$ over $C_n$; a chain map $C \to C'$ need not carry a chosen $p$ to a
chosen $p'$. Corollary 3.4 uses only the commutative ladder of the sequences,
not any compatible splitting, so non-naturality of the splitting does not
obstruct it.
(c) Corollary 3.4: a chain map between chain complexes of free abelian groups
inducing an isomorphism on homology induces an isomorphism on cohomology with
any coefficient group $G$. Proof: naturality gives a commuting ladder whose rows
are the two universal-coefficient sequences, padded with zeros to five terms.
The five maps are $0$, $(\alpha_*)^*$ on the Ext terms, $\alpha^*$ in the middle,
$(\alpha_*)^*$ on the Hom terms, and $0$. Since $\alpha_*$ is an isomorphism on
homology, both outer non-trivial maps are isomorphisms, and the five lemma gives
that $\alpha^*$ is one too.
(d) The complex of at2-01 Problem 3 with $G = \mathbb{Z}$ has
$H^2 = \mathbb{Z}_2$ while $\operatorname{Hom}(H_2,\mathbb{Z}) = 0$. The same
complex with $G = \mathbb{Q}$ (or any field of characteristic other than $2$)
has $\operatorname{Ext} = 0$ in every degree, so $h$ is an isomorphism in every
degree even though $H_1(C) = \mathbb{Z}_2$ is torsion. The distinguishing
feature is not whether the complex has torsion but whether the coefficient group
sees it: Ext vanishes exactly when the relevant multiplications are invertible
on $G$.
</details>
