**1.** Solve (4,−3,1,7) + 2x = (5,9,−6,8) for x ∈ ℝ⁴.

$x$ is effectively another tuple of the form $(x1, x2, x3, x4)$. Hence

$4+2x=5$ and $x=1/2$
$-3+2x=9$ and $x=6$
$1+2x=-6$ and $x=7/2$
$7+2x=8$ and $x=1/2$


**2.** Prove −(−v) = v.

Let $v \in V$ . By the definition of additive inverse we get:

$v+(-v)=0=-(-v)+(-v)$

Adding $-(-v)$ to both sides gives

$v+(-v)+(-(-v))=-(-v)+(-v)+(-(-v))$

Simplifying both sides gives

$v=-(-v)$


**3.** Prove: if av = 0 (a ∈ 𝔽, v ∈ V), then a = 0 or v = 0.

If $a=0$ then we are done by definition. Suppose $a\neq0$ then

$v=1v=\frac{1}{a}(av)=\frac{1}{a}\times 0=0$




**4.** Prove the empty set is not a vector space.

The empty set has no $0$ vector such that $v+0=v$ because it contains no elements. This fails one of the required rules for a vector space.

**6.** Is ℝ ∪ {∞} ∪ {−∞} a vector space (with the "obvious" extended-arithmetic rules)? Prove your answer.

Okay, this exercise is from Axler (as are the others) but this version is very, very poorly explained. It would have been better to reproduce Axler in full.

Nonetheless, the answer is no. Let $t\in R$ then

$t+(\infty)+(-\infty)=(\infty)+(-\infty)=0\neq t=t+0=t+((\infty)+(-\infty))$


This does not satisfy associativity of addition needed for a vector space.
