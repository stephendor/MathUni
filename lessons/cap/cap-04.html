The written defence (cap-04)

Write it so that a competent reader who distrusts you can either reproduce it or find the flaw.

That is the whole specification, and it rules out both of the documents people actually write. It rules out the one that reports what worked, because a reader cannot find a flaw in a description that has been smoothed. And it rules out the one that hedges everything, because a reader cannot reproduce a claim that was never stated.

This is the last unit of the curriculum, and it has the smallest amount of new mathematics in it: almost everything it needs has already been proved. What it adds is the obligation to write down which of those results carries your conclusion — and, twice in this particular case, that the answer is "none of them, yet".

Prediction gate — commit before reading on. Your defence must state "the stability guarantee that licenses the claim". For the TDL pipeline as it currently stands, predict what that section can honestly contain.

The available guarantee is real but narrow: Theorem 6.3(a) with Definition 6.1 gives a bottleneck bound, hence a persistence threshold 2ε, licensing claims about features above that threshold. The pipeline does not state its claim in that form, and cannot yet, because ε — the covering radius of the landmark subsample — is computed inside maxmin_landmarks and never returned. The third option is the failure this unit exists to prevent: an unavailable guarantee is a thing to state, not a section to delete, because the deletion is invisible to the reader and the statement is not.

Why this matters for the mission: The defence states the claim, the topological evidence, the stability guarantee that licenses it, and the failure modes ruled out — pw-05's critique standard turned on your own work.

Segment 1 · ~25 min

Four parts, and the standard they are scored against

The mission strip fixes the structure. Take each part by what it leaves the reader able to do: after the claim, state what would have to be observed for it to be false; after the evidence, locate every number in a file on disk; after the stability guarantee, name the theorem carrying the inference and the hypotheses it needs; after the failure modes, name what you considered and rejected, and by what artifact.

A note on paths. Every TDL/… path in this unit refers to the TDL project, a separate repository at C:\Users\steph\TDL as named in MISSION.md — not to anything inside MathUni. So TDL/results/trajectory_tda_integration/03_ph.json and TDL/trajectory_tda/topology/trajectory_ph.py will not resolve from a checkout of this repository, and are not meant to. That is the point of the capstone: the object under examination is the research project, and this repository holds only the mathematics used to examine it.

The standard this unit was told to use does not exist here. The mission strip says "pw-05's critique standard". pw-05 is Proof style: writing and critiquing, and it has no lesson; its two resources in curriculum/syllabus.yaml are "Cummings appendix" and "Expository Writing folder". This unit's own second resource is that same folder. It is not in this repository, not in resources/bookmap.json, and not listed in resources/RESOURCES.md; and the Cummings volume in the bookmap runs from Chapter 1 to Introduction to Group Theory with no appendix. So the standard cannot be read, and nothing here is attributed to it. What this unit uses instead is a critique standard that is in this repository and can be checked line by line: curriculum/LESSON-RUBRIC.md, the rubric every lesson in this curriculum — including this one — was admitted by. The substitution is stated rather than quietly made, which is itself the habit the unit is about.

The rubric, and what makes it a standard rather than a checklist

Three of its design decisions are worth taking whole. First, the gates are ordered: "The four gates are ordered. A lesson must clear each to reach the next." Cheap mechanical checks run before expensive human ones, so a defect that a script can find never costs a reader's attention. Second, one gate has a veto — Gate 2, mathematical correctness: "Any single defect here fails the lesson outright, whatever Gates 1/3 look like", because "a lesson that teaches a false statement is worse than no lesson". Third, and most useful here, the rubric states its own limits: "Gate 0 proves form and mention, never truth."

That third one has a companion warning that transposes directly to a defence. On declared gaps the rubric says: "a candidate that declares zero gaps is not thereby complete — the likelier reading is that it filled silently from recollection… Zero gaps plus full coverage is a claim to be checked, not a result." Read "gaps" as "limitations" and you have the rule for reading a defence: a document with no limitations section has not been checked, it has been asserted.

Self-check 1. Why does the rubric put the veto on Gate 2 rather than on Gate 0, which is the gate that cannot be argued with?

The rubric says so in its own warning under Gate 0: it "proves form and mention, never truth". A citation that resolves, a number that appears in a file, a section that is present — none of these is evidence that the sentence built from them is true. Ordering is about cost, not authority: cheap checks run first so that expensive attention is not spent on documents that fail them. And the third option inverts the rubric's actual position, which is that Gate 0 failures are binary rejections precisely because they are unarguable.

Self-check 2. A defence's limitations section is empty. Under the transposed standard, what does that establish?

The rubric's reasoning on gap counts is that zero is the reading you would get both from work with no gaps and from work whose gaps were filled silently, so the count alone cannot distinguish them — which makes it a claim requiring evidence rather than evidence in itself. The second option is exactly the inference the warning blocks. The third understates it: on a defence, an absent limitations section leaves every inference in the document unchecked against its own hypotheses, which is Gate 2 territory and therefore a veto, not a score.

Segment 2 · ~25 min

Part 3: the guarantee that holds, and the ones that do not

Part 3 is the hard part and the reason the other three are worth writing. Start with what is available, because there genuinely is something.

Definition 6.1 (Čech and Rips distances). For finite point sets P and Q with appropriate metrics, dRips(P,Q) = maxk dB(Dgmkℛ(P), Dgmkℛ(Q)), and similarly for dCech with the Čech filtration. Definition 6.2 (Hausdorff distance). For compact A, B ⊆ (Z, dZ), dH(A,B) = max{maxa∈A dZ(a,B), maxb∈B dZ(b,A)}. — Dey & Wang §6.1, Definitions 6.1 and 6.2, p. 181

Theorem 6.3(a). Given two finite sets P, Q ⊆ (Z, dZ), dCech(P,Q) ≤ dH(P,Q) and dRips(P,Q) ≤ dH(P,Q). Part (b) replaces dH by the Gromov–Hausdorff distance for two finite metric spaces, with an extra factor 2 on the Čech side; the text remarks that (b) extends to totally bounded metric spaces, which need not be finite. — Dey & Wang §6.1, Theorem 6.3 and the remark following it, p. 182

Worked example: the guarantee, in full. Suppose dH(P, X) ≤ ε.

Step 1. Theorem 6.3(a) gives dRips(P, X) ≤ ε, and Definition 6.1 says that left-hand side is maxk dB. So for every k, dB(Dgmkℛ(P), Dgmkℛ(X)) ≤ ε.

Step 2. A bottleneck matching may send a diagram point to the diagonal at cost equal to its L∞-distance to it. The nearest diagonal point to (b,d) is the midpoint ((b+d)/2, (b+d)/2), at L∞-distance (d−b)/2 — half the persistence.

Step 3. So a bar of persistence 2ε costs exactly ε to delete, and a matching realising dB ≤ ε is permitted to delete it. Only features of persistence strictly greater than 2ε may be claimed.

This is cap-01's derivation, restated because part 3 is where it gets used.

That is a real guarantee, and it licenses a real form of claim: a statement about features above an explicit threshold. It is worth being clear about what makes it usable — it converts a fact about your sampling into a number, and the number is checkable.

Which ε, and about what. Theorem 6.3(a) quantifies over two finite sets in a common metric space, so the second argument decides what you have proved. Put the full embedded cloud there and ε is the covering radius of the landmark subsample: both sets are finite, the theorem applies verbatim, and the claim is about the subsampling. Put a hidden space there and it is not finite, so (a) does not literally cover it — the remark after Theorem 6.3 extends (b), the Gromov–Hausdorff part with its factor 2 for Čech, to totally bounded spaces, which is a different statement in a different metric. These are two different claims with two different ε's, and only one of them has an ε any computation can produce. A defence that writes "ε" without saying which has skipped the hypothesis.

The number the code computes and throws away

03_ph.json records n_landmarks = 5000, an elapsed time, and the per-dimension summaries. It does not record a covering radius. Now open TDL/trajectory_tda/topology/trajectory_ph.py and read maxmin_landmarks: it maintains an array min_dists holding, for each of the N points, its distance to the nearest landmark chosen so far, and updates it on every iteration — because taking the argmax of that array is how the next landmark is chosen. Its maximum at loop exit is the covering radius of every landmark but the last, an upper bound on the true one; one further update, the same O(N) pass the loop already performs each iteration, makes it exact. Either way the number is there, and the function returns indices, landmarks.

So the single hypothesis on which the whole guarantee turns is one line away and absent. Two things stop this being a cheap complaint. First, note what it costs: without ε there is no threshold, so part 3's first paragraph cannot be completed and must be written conditionally. Second, note where the mechanism already lives — TDL/trajectory_tda/topology/permutation_nulls.py computes and records covering_radius_at_n_perm on the deduplication path, choosing the permutation count via compute_greedy_dedup_count so that, as its docstring puts it, "the covering radius is at or below" a documented tolerance. The project records covering radii; it records them on the null path and not on the path the headline persistence result comes through. A finding written as "nobody thought of this" is both ruder and less accurate than one written as "this is recorded over there and not here", and only the second tells anyone what to do.

Visual: which links in the chain close

Press the button. Two links are amber, not red: a theorem applies to them and its input is simply unrecorded, so one line of code moves both to green. The red links are different in kind. The embedding is constrained by no theorem at all — cap-02's ledger records "none" against that row, and 07_umap_sensitivity.json is what that empty cell looks like once measured: an adjusted Rand index of about 0.310 between the PCA and UMAP clusterings. And the last link is cap-02's junction 2: Theorem 13.2 requires a bound on dW,1, everything upstream supplies a bound on dB, and by Definition 13.8 a Wasserstein distance is a sum over matched pairs where the bottleneck distance is a maximum, so the inequality needed runs the wrong way. That link closes only by changing the design: Theorem 13.1's landscape bound, Λ∞ ≤ dB with constant 1, accepts exactly what the upstream stages produce.

Junction 1 of cap-02 — sampling to sparsification — is the one that does not close by any choice: Theorem 6.4's sparsification error is log(1/(1−ε)), an increment at log scale, while Theorem 6.3's is an increment on the linear scale, and adding them is adding a length to a ratio. This pipeline subsamples by maxmin landmarks rather than by Definition 6.5's sparse Rips filtration, so Theorem 6.4 is not what governs it — which is itself a line the defence owes: name the stage, name the theorem that governs it, and where none does, say so.

Self-check 3. The covering radius is unrecorded. What is the honest content of part 3's first paragraph?

The theorem does hold regardless, which is exactly why the second option is tempting and wrong: what the theorem gives is an implication whose antecedent is a number, and without the number you have the implication and no conclusion. Writing it conditionally keeps the implication visible and tells the reader precisely what to supply. The third option quietly moves the missing hypothesis to a section readers skim, which is the difference between a defence and a report.

Self-check 4. Why are two links amber and two red?

The distinction is between a missing measurement and a missing theorem, and it matters because the two have completely different costs: one is a line of code, the other is a design change or a piece of research. Nothing in the diagram claims anything about the size of the errors — an unrecorded ε could be tiny or ruinous, and that is the point of recording it. The third option matches neither: the embedding row is in cap-02's ledger and measured by an artifact, and it is red on both counts.

Segment 3 · ~25 min

Parts 1, 2 and 4, and failing your own draft

Guided derivation

Prompt. 03_ph.json reports, for H1, 5962 features of which 5961 are finite, with total persistence 2224.68; and for H0, 5000 features of which 4999 are finite, with total persistence 20411.13. (1) Compute the mean finite bar length in each dimension. (2) Deduce the largest ε under which the typical H1 bar could be claimed at all. (3) Say what follows for a statistic that sums over all 5961 finite H1 bars — and state carefully what it does not follow.

Hint: the threshold is on each bar individually. Ask what the statistic is made of.

Click to reveal model answer

(1) The means. H0: 20411.13 / 4999 ≈ 4.083. H1: 2224.68 / 5961 ≈ 0.373. Both are in the embedding's metric, whatever that metric is — which is a hypothesis the defence must state, since a threshold in an unnamed metric is not a threshold.

(2) The ceiling. A bar may be claimed only if its persistence exceeds 2ε. For the mean H1 bar, 0.373 > 2ε requires ε < 0.187. For the longest H1 bar, at max persistence 3.213, the ceiling is ε < 1.607; for the longest H0 bar, at 15.809, it is ε < 7.904. So the longest features are robust to a fairly coarse subsample and the typical ones are not, and the gap between 0.187 and 1.607 is the range in which the answer depends entirely on the number nobody recorded.

(3) What follows. Total persistence is a sum over all 5961 finite H1 bars. Unless ε is below roughly 0.19, the typical summand is a bar that Theorem 6.3(a) with Definition 6.1 does not permit you to claim individually — the matching realising dB ≤ ε is allowed to delete it. So the statistic is, in the regime the defence has not ruled out, dominated by bars that carry no individual licence.

What this does not show. It does not show the statistic is invalid. A sum over individually unclaimable bars can be a perfectly good test statistic: the permutation test compares the observed sum against the sums produced by surrogates through the identical pipeline, and that comparison is licensed by the test's own logic, which never required any bar to be individually claimable. Nor does it show the p-values are wrong. What it shows is narrower and sharper: the defence may not describe this statistic as topological evidence licensed by the stability theorem, because the licence covers individual features above a threshold and the statistic is not one. It is licensed by a different argument, and the defence should name which.

Why this is the derivation part 3 needs. It takes three numbers already in the artifact, does arithmetic anyone can check, and converts a vague worry — "is the subsample fine enough?" — into a decision boundary at ε ≈ 0.19. That is what a stability section is for: not to invoke a theorem, but to produce a number a reader can compare against.

Part 1, and why it comes last in the writing

Write part 3 first and part 1 second. What you can license determines what you may claim, and doing it the other way round produces a claim you then have to walk back — which reads, to a hostile reader, exactly like a claim that was walked back. A model fragment, in cap-01's form:

"In the embedded trajectory data (n = 27280 trajectories; embedding named from the parameter ledger, since 03_ph.json does not record it), the H1 persistence structure of a 5000-point maxmin landmark subsample differs from that of second-order Markov surrogates by more than the surrogates differ from each other, measured by mean 2-Wasserstein distance between diagrams, at the 0.05 level. This claim is not established: the test reports p = 0.078."

A claim you did not establish still belongs in part 1, stated as the claim you tested. A defence that states only the claims that came out well is not a defence.

The two failures, and they are symmetric. One is the smoothed report: everything worked, the limitations are generic, and the reader cannot find the flaw because the surface has no seams. The other is the confession: every finding hedged, the limits section longer than the results, and the reader cannot reproduce anything because no claim was ever made. Both are unfalsifiable documents, which is why they feel so different and fail the same way. The specification in the hook rules out both by requiring two things of the same text — that a reader can reproduce it, which needs a stated claim, and that they can find the flaw, which needs stated limits. cap-03's discipline is what holds them together: every claim carries its "does not imply" line, and the limits are as specific as the results.

You try (solo). (i) Write part 3 in full: the conditional guarantee with its threshold and its named hypothesis, then the links that do not close, each with what would close it and what it would cost. (ii) Write part 1 as a claim in cap-01's form, chosen so that part 3 licenses it or so that the gap is stated. (iii) Complete the failure-mode ledger to at least six rows, including one failure mode of the defence rather than of the analysis. (iv) Score your own draft on the four transposed gates, recording at least one Gate 2 defect — or explaining, in a sentence a sceptic would accept, why there is none. (v) Write the closing paragraph: what a reader who distrusts you should check first, and what they would find.

Hint: for (v), the cheapest thing to check is the missing number, and pointing a hostile reader at it is stronger than waiting for them to find it.

Self-check 5. A ledger row reads "ruled out" and its third column says "the effect is implausible given the design". What is wrong?

The ledger's whole function is to let a reader check the rejections rather than take them, so a row whose evidence is the author's judgement returns the reader to trusting the author. A design argument may be perfectly correct and still belongs in a different column — as the reason a test was not run, with the row's status "open". Deleting the row is worse than either: it removes the failure mode from the reader's view, which is the one outcome the ledger exists to prevent.

Self-check 6. MISSION.md lists as a success criterion: "I can explain the stability theorem for persistence diagrams and why it licenses the TDL pipeline's conclusions." What does this unit's analysis do to that criterion?

The criterion asks for an explanation of what the theorem licenses; an accurate explanation that ends in "and therefore not this statistic, for these reasons" is a better answer than an inaccurate one that ends in "and therefore this statistic". Reading it as a failure treats a presupposition in the phrasing as a requirement on the answer — the mission document was written before the analysis, which is the normal order. And the third option misses that the criterion is explicitly about the pipeline, not the theorem alone.

Exit ritual: blank-page reconstruction

Close the page, look away, and reconstruct the argument skeleton — three load-bearing claims and why each forces the next:

  1. The four parts of the defence, and the four gates they are scored against.
  2. The guarantee that holds, its threshold, and the hypothesis it needs.
  3. Which links do not close, which of those is a missing measurement rather than a missing theorem, and what the ledger's "ruled out" requires.
