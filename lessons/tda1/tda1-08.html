Morse theory and sublevel-set persistence (tda1-08)

Watch the sublevel sets of a function fill up; topology only changes at critical points (an-12's derivatives return).

Predict before we start. Almost every smooth function on a compact manifold is Morse — being Morse is generic. What is the situation for piecewise linear functions on a triangulated manifold?

Edelsbrunner states it flatly on p. 164: "In contrast to the smooth case, PL Morse functions are not dense among the class of all PL functions." The PL monkey saddle is his example, and the consequence he draws is that "it is therefore not reasonable to assume a PL Morse function as input". Segment 2 works out what that costs, and Segment 3 what it costs the mission strip.

Why this matters for the mission: Sublevel-set persistence links Morse theory to filtrations — how a scalar field becomes a barcode.

The link the strip names is real and the source makes it explicitly — Chapter VII lists "the lower star filtration of a piecewise linear function in Section VI.3" among its examples of filtrations (p. 179). What needs care is the second half. A scalar field measured on data is not a PL function on a triangulated manifold, and the steps between them — choosing a triangulation, checking genericity, deciding what to do when the function is not PL Morse, and knowing whether the answer depends on the triangulation — are mostly not in these pages. Segment 3 lists them and says for each where it is settled, which for several is nowhere here.

Segment 1 · ~25 min

Smooth Morse theory, in the two theorems persistence uses

Let 𝕄 be a smooth d-manifold. A smooth f : 𝕄 → ℝ induces a linear map Dfx : T𝕄x → ℝ on each tangent space, and a linear map to ℝ has image either all of ℝ or {0} — so every point is either regular (Dfx surjective) or critical (Dfx zero), with no third case. In local coordinates, critical means every partial derivative vanishes. This is an-12's stationary-point condition on a manifold — Edelsbrunner §VI.1, p. 151.

Second derivatives sort the critical points. The Hessian H(x) is the matrix of second partials, and a critical point is non-degenerate when det H(x) ≠ 0 — the multivariable form of an-12's second-derivative test, which in one variable asks only that f″ ≠ 0. Degeneracy is not exotic: f(x₁) = x₁³ has a degenerate critical point at 0, and f(x₁, x₂) = x₁³ − 3x₁x₂² has one at the origin, the monkey saddle, so called because the surface goes up and down three times around it — room for two legs and a tail — p. 152.

Morse Lemma — Edelsbrunner, p. 152

Let u be a non-degenerate critical point of f : 𝕄 → ℝ. There are local coordinates with u = (0, …, 0) such that f(x) = f(u) − x₁² − ⋯ − xq² + xq+1² + ⋯ + xd² in a small neighbourhood of u.

The number of minus signs is the index, and it sorts non-degenerate critical points into d+1 types — for a surface: minima (0), saddles (1), maxima (2). Two consequences. Locally f − f(u) is a non-degenerate quadratic form, which vanishes only at the origin, so non-degenerate critical points are isolated; on a compact manifold there are therefore finitely many. And a function can fail to have any index at all: the height function of a torus lying on its side has an entire circle of minima and another of maxima, all degenerate.

Morse function — Edelsbrunner, p. 152

A smooth function f : 𝕄 → ℝ on a manifold such that (i) all critical points are non-degenerate, and (ii) the critical points have distinct function values.

Edelsbrunner keeps both conditions throughout and notes that (ii) is sometimes dropped. His torus examples are worth holding on to as a measure of how generic this is: for a geometrically perfect torus the height function satisfies (i) for all but two directions — those parallel to the symmetry axis — and violates (ii) for two circles of directions, those along which the two saddles have equal height. The exceptions are a measure-zero set; being Morse is the normal case.

Two structural results follow, and they are what persistence uses. The gradient ∇f is defined by ⟨X(x), ∇f(x)⟩ = X[f] for all vector fields X — which needs a Riemannian metric, since without an inner product there is no way to convert the derivative into a vector. Following its flow gives a 1-parameter group of diffeomorphisms, and it carries sublevel sets into sublevel sets. Hence:

No change away from critical points; a handle at each one — Edelsbrunner, pp. 154

If a < b and f−1[a, b] is compact and contains no critical points, then 𝕄a is diffeomorphic to 𝕄b. If instead it contains exactly one critical point, of index q, then 𝕄b has the homotopy type of 𝕄a with a q-handle attached — 𝔹q glued along 𝕊q−1 by a map into bd 𝕄a, with q = 0 meaning simply the addition of a disjoint point, since 𝕊−1 is empty.

Worked, then fading: the upright torus, one handle at a time. — Edelsbrunner, p. 151 and Figure VI.2

Stand a torus upright and take the height function. It has four critical points: u a minimum, v and w saddles, z a maximum. Walk the sublevel set up through them.

Self-check 1. Notice the asymmetry in that pair of statements: away from critical points, diffeomorphic; across one, the homotopy type of. Why the weaker conclusion in the second case?

The flow argument gives a diffeomorphism because it can be integrated across the whole interval; at a critical point the flow stalls and there is nothing to integrate. What survives is that 𝕄b deformation retracts onto 𝕄a with a cell attached — the smooth structure of the result depends on the gluing map, but its homotopy type does not. This asymmetry is why the whole subject computes with homology rather than with diffeomorphism type.

Counting critical points then bounds Betti numbers. Writing cq for the number of index-q critical points, the Morse Inequalities say cq ≥ βq(𝕄) for all q (weak), and Σq≤j(−1)j−qcq ≥ Σq≤j(−1)j−qβq(𝕄) for all j (strong). At j = d the strong inequality is an equality, and both sides are then the Euler characteristic — tda1-02's Euler–Poincaré Theorem on the right, and the alternating count of critical points on the left — §VI.2, p. 159.

Segment 2 · ~25 min

The PL case, where the genericity fails

"We rarely find smooth functions in practical situations." A PL function on a simplicial complex K is determined by values at vertices and extended linearly, f(x) = Σᵢ bᵢ(x)f(uᵢ) with the barycentric coordinates of tda1-01. Call f generic when the vertex values are distinct, and order f(u₁) < ⋯ < f(un). Let Ki be the full subcomplex on the first i vertices: σ ∈ Ki exactly when every vertex of σ has index at most i — §VI.3, p. 162.

Lower star and lower star filtration — Edelsbrunner, p. 162

St−ui = {σ ∈ St ui : x ∈ σ ⇒ f(x) ≤ f(ui)}, the simplices of the star for which ui is the maximum vertex. By genericity each simplex has a unique maximum vertex, so the lower stars partition K and Ki is the union of the first i of them. The nested sequence ∅ = K₀ ⊆ K₁ ⊆ ⋯ ⊆ Kn = K is the lower star filtration of f.

Check it against tda1-01's Definition 2.1 and every clause holds, including both that "filtration of K" adds: the union is Kn = K and the intersection is K₀ = ∅. Unlike Oudot's own octahedron example, this one satisfies the empty-intersection clause by construction.

The filtration is a discretization, and the next result says nothing is lost. For f(ui) ≤ a < f(ui+1), the sublevel set |K|a has the same homotopy type as |Ki|. The proof is a deformation retraction: a simplex with vertices on both sides is the join of its maximal face in Ki and its maximal face outside, hence a union of segments from x ∈ |Ki| to y outside; the sublevel set contains the portion up to the point of value a, and (1 − t)y + tx sweeps that portion down to x as t goes from 0 to 1 — p. 163.

That is a statement about homotopy type, and one more ingredient converts it into what persistence needs: at1-07's Corollary 2.11, that homotopy equivalences induce isomorphisms on homology. With it, H∗(|K|a) ≅ H∗(Ki) — and, since the retractions are compatible with the inclusions, the whole persistence module of the continuous sublevel-set family agrees with that of the lower star filtration. Without it one would have two families that look alike and no theorem relating them.

The classification is by the lower link Lk−ui, the simplices of the link whose vertices all have smaller value. Going from Ki−1 to Ki attaches the closed lower star, glued along the lower link. If K triangulates a d-manifold this restricts things sharply, because every vertex star is an open d-ball and every vertex link a (d−1)-sphere. Reading the reduced Betti numbers of the lower link off the picture gives Table VI.1: regular (0, 0, 0), minimum (1, 0, 0), saddle (0, 1, 0), maximum (0, 0, 1) — with β̃−1 = 1 for the empty lower link, the one exception to β̃₀ = β₀ − 1 — pp. 163–164.

PL critical vertices and PL Morse functions — Edelsbrunner, p. 164

ui is PL regular if its lower link is non-empty but homologically trivial, and a simple PL critical vertex of index q if its lower link has the reduced homology of the (q−1)-sphere — that is, β̃q−1 = 1 and all other reduced Betti numbers zero. A PL function on a manifold is PL Morse if (i) every vertex is PL regular or simple PL critical, and (ii) the vertex values are distinct.

Self-check 2. "PL regular" requires the lower link to be non-empty. Why is that clause there rather than being a harmless extra?

The empty lower link has β̃−1 = 1, which is the reduced homology of the (−1)-sphere — so by the definition of a simple PL critical vertex it is critical of index 0, a minimum. Dropping "non-empty" would classify every minimum as regular and lose every 0-dimensional birth. The attachment is perfectly well defined: it adds a disjoint cone, which is the PL analogue of the smooth q = 0 case's disjoint point.

And here the smooth analogy breaks. Edelsbrunner: "In contrast to the smooth case, PL Morse functions are not dense among the class of all PL functions. Equivalently, a PL function on a manifold may require a substantial perturbation before it becomes PL Morse." His example is the PL monkey saddle, whose centre vertex has a lower link with two components rather than one — a 2-fold saddle. In general a k-fold saddle on a 2-manifold has β̃₀ = k, and it can be unfolded into k simple saddles by introducing k − 1 new vertices with values close to the original. His own conclusion is the operative one: "it is therefore not reasonable to assume a PL Morse function as input". For higher-dimensional manifolds he says only that it is "less clear" how to unfold.

Notice what unfolding does: it changes the triangulation. Whether the unfolded function has the same persistence diagram as the original is a further question, and nothing on these pages answers it. tda1-05's Persistence Equivalence Theorem would settle it given isomorphisms between the two sequences of homology groups commuting with everything — but those isomorphisms are exactly what would have to be exhibited, and Edelsbrunner does not exhibit them.

The PL Morse Inequalities have the same shape — cq ≥ βq(K) weak, the alternating version strong — and this time Edelsbrunner proves them, by induction along the lower star filtration using at1-09's Mayer–Vietoris sequence with K = Ki, K′ = Ki−1, K″ = St−ui ∪ Lk−ui and A = Lk−ui. K″ is a cone, hence homologically trivial, which collapses the sequence and gives rank H̃p(Ki) = rank H̃p(Ki−1) − kp + kp−1 — p. 166. He omits the proof of the smooth strong inequalities and refers instead to this one — a nice inversion, since the PL case is the one where genericity fails and the elementary proof is available.

Segment 3 · ~30 min

From a function to a barcode, link by link

The bridge to tda1-03 is one definition. That unit's filtration comes from a monotonic function on simplices, f(σ) ≤ f(τ) whenever σ is a face of τ. Given a generic PL function on vertices, set f(σ) = max{f(u) : u a vertex of σ}. A face has a subset of the vertices, so its maximum is no larger — monotonic. Its sublevel complexes are exactly the Ki, and its compatible orderings are exactly the orderings that list each lower star together. So the lower star filtration is a tda1-03 filtration, and everything there applies: the persistence algorithm, the pairing, the diagram.

Reveal the model answer

Licensed here, and by what. (1) A PL function on a triangulated manifold has a lower star filtration — §VI.3's definition, needing genericity. (2) That filtration is a filtration in tda1-01's Definition 2.1, all clauses. (3) The sublevel sets |K|a are homotopy equivalent to the |Ki| — §VI.3's deformation retraction — hence have isomorphic homology, by at1-07's Corollary 2.11. (4) The vertex-indexed filtration is the filtration of an explicit monotonic function on simplices, so tda1-03 applies and produces a diagram. (5) Critical vertices are classified by the reduced homology of the lower link, so births and deaths can be read off local data. Chapter VII names the lower star filtration among its filtrations (p. 179), so the source itself makes the join.

Not licensed here. (a) Getting a triangulated manifold at all. Data is not a manifold and is not triangulated; choosing K is a modelling decision these pages do not discuss. Edelsbrunner's Chapter III gives Delaunay and alpha complexes, outside this unit. (b) Genericity. Measured values collide; the definition requires distinct vertex values and nothing here says what to do when they do not, beyond assuming they do not. (c) PL Morse-ness. Edelsbrunner says outright that it is not reasonable to assume it, and unfolding is offered only for 2-manifolds. (d) Independence of the triangulation. Two triangulations of the same space give two filtrations; whether they give the same diagram is not addressed here, and tda1-02 already recorded that Edelsbrunner calls the corresponding statement for ordinary homology "difficult" and refers it elsewhere. (e) Stability of any of it under measurement noise. That is tda1-07's, and tda1-07 recorded its own limits.

The honest form of the strip. Sublevel-set persistence links Morse theory to filtrations, and the lower star filtration is the bridge: it turns a generic PL function into a filtration whose persistence is the sublevel-set persistence of the function. Getting from a measured scalar field to such a function is a separate chain of modelling steps, most of which are outside these pages.

Self-check 3. The hook says "topology only changes at critical points". That is the smooth statement. What is the PL analogue, and is it the same claim?

Smoothly, the conclusion is that 𝕄a and 𝕄b are diffeomorphic — the strongest possible sameness. In the PL setting, a PL regular vertex has a homologically trivial lower link, so passing it leaves the reduced homology unchanged, which is strictly less than saying the complexes are the same. And the classification into regular and simple critical presupposes PL Morse-ness: for a k-fold saddle the vertex is neither regular nor simple critical, so the hook's sentence has no subject. Both qualifications matter and neither is visible in the hook.

You try. Take K to be the boundary of an octahedron, triangulating 𝕊², with the height function taking distinct values at the six vertices. Compute the lower link of each vertex, classify it by Table VI.1, and check the count against the weak PL Morse Inequalities using β₀(𝕊²) = β₂(𝕊²) = 1 and β₁(𝕊²) = 0. Then check the strong inequality at j = 2 and confirm it is an equality with χ(𝕊²) = 2.

Reveal the skeleton

1. Smoothly, index sorts critical points and the sublevel set changes only by handle attachment. Dfx is surjective or zero, so points are regular or critical; the Hessian sorts the critical ones, and the Morse Lemma puts a non-degenerate one in normal form with q minus signs. Non-degenerate critical points are isolated, hence finite on a compact manifold. Away from critical values the sublevel sets are diffeomorphic; across one of index q, a q-handle is attached and only the homotopy type is determined. Counting gives the Morse Inequalities, an equality at j = d where both sides are the Euler characteristic.

2. In the PL case the filtration is the lower star filtration, and the genericity fails. A generic PL function orders the vertices; Ki is the full subcomplex on the first i, the lower stars partition K, and |K|a deformation retracts to |Ki| — which becomes a statement about homology only via at1-07's Corollary 2.11. Vertices are classified by the reduced Betti numbers of the lower link, with the empty lower link a minimum rather than a regular vertex. And PL Morse functions are not dense: the PL monkey saddle needs unfolding, unfolding changes the triangulation, and nothing here says the diagram survives it.

3. The bridge to persistence is a monotonic function, and the chain beyond it is longer than it looks. Setting f(σ) to the maximum vertex value makes the lower star filtration a tda1-03 filtration, so the algorithm and the diagram apply, and Chapter VII names it as such. What these pages do not supply: a triangulation of measured data, a guarantee of genericity, PL Morse-ness, independence of the triangulation, or stability under noise. The strip is right about the link and silent about the five steps before it.
