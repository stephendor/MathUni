Mini-project: a full TDA analysis (lab-09)

Six hundred points on a torus. Two loops and one void, and the answer is known in advance, which is the only reason this unit can say what it says. Four thresholds get applied to the same barcode. Quoting the noise standard deviation as δ gives 0.12 and admits all six leading bars — and is wrong, because a Gaussian is unbounded and 488 of the 600 points moved further than that. lab-05's stability rule, applied to the measured displacement bound δ = 0.1312, gives 0.5248 and admits three. The coordinate-permutation null, the strongest control this module has built, admits five. The largest-gap heuristic — lab-01's ratio of 98.1, which lab-05 examined and declined to license — gives two, and gives two at every seed tried. The rule that gets the right answer is the one with no theorem behind it, and the deliverable of this unit is not the answer. It is the paragraph that says so.

Predict before we start. Your data is sampled with Gaussian noise of standard deviation 0.03. You apply lab-05's rule that a bar of lifetime greater than 4δ cannot be an artefact of that noise. What is δ?

Not determined. lab-05's δ must bound every point's Euclidean displacement, and a standard deviation is a scale parameter of a distribution with no maximum — no multiple of it bounds anything with certainty. The size of the gap is worth seeing rather than reasoning about: on this sample 488 of the 600 points moved further than 0.03, and the largest moved 0.131206. Quoting σ as δ understates the bound by a factor of 4.4 here, and by an unbounded factor in principle.

The second option is the sophisticated version of the same error. It corrects for dimension — the expected displacement of a 3-D isotropic Gaussian is indeed larger than one coordinate's σ — but a corrected typical value is still not a bound, and 0.052 would be exceeded by plenty of points too.

This unit can measure δ, because the generator is ours and the clean sample can be kept: δ = 0.131206, so 4δ = 0.5248, which admits three of the six leading bars rather than all six. Note what makes that available — not that the noise level is known, but that the realisation is. On real data neither is, which is Segment 3's subject. The first option is what this lesson asserted in draft, and two reviewers caught it independently.

Why this matters for the mission: A dry run of the capstone — the whole pipeline, on data you choose, defended in writing.

The strip is accurate, and "data you choose" is doing something this unit cannot do for you.

"The whole pipeline" — yes, and it is walked once, end to end. Point cloud with no topology (lab-01), Rips with its conventions stated (lab-02), the reduction and the barcode (lab-03, lab-04), the threshold (lab-05), the landscape (lab-06), the controls (lab-07), the parameter discipline (lab-08).

"Data you choose" is where this unit and the capstone part company. The data here is a synthetic torus with a known answer and a δ written into the generator. Both are luxuries. The known answer is what makes Segment 2 possible at all — it is the only reason anyone can say that the gap heuristic is the rule that works here — and the known δ removes lab-05's single modelling assumption for free. Real data supplies neither. Segment 3 goes through the unit's five conclusions and marks each one survives, survives with a stated assumption, or does not transfer.

"Defended in writing" is the deliverable, and it is the harder half. The analysis is twenty lines of code. The defence is a conclusion paragraph and a disclosure paragraph, and the disclosure has to be sufficient for a hostile reader to see which conclusions rest on a theorem and which on a heuristic. No syllabus action; the strip is right and this lesson is what it asks for.

Not claimed here. That the points lie on a torus, or that any bar "is" a hole in a surface — every theorem in this module relates a dataset to its own diagram, never a dataset to a space it came from, and that restriction is not relaxed on the last page.
Segment 1 · 25 min

The computation, with its conventions, and its null

Six hundred points on a torus with R = 2, r = 1, Gaussian noise of standard deviation 0.03. Alongside it, built before anything is computed, the null: the same cloud with each coordinate independently permuted. Same means, same standard deviations, and the sorted x-coordinates are literally the same array. Everything but the joint structure is preserved exactly.

Worked — one computation, both clouds. 
torus H1   [1.2965 1.2373 0.5997 0.5238 0.5233 0.495 ]
null  H1   [0.4192 0.3798 0.3684 0.3614 0.3442 0.3372]
torus H2   [0.6845 0.1078 0.1058 0.0943 0.0907 0.0861]
null  H2   [0.2195 0.1847 0.1693 0.1582 0.1503 0.1498]
A torus has b₀ = 1, b₁ = 2, b₂ = 1.

The H₂ column is the cleanest signal in the module — 0.6845 against a next-longest of 0.1078, a factor of 6.3, with the null topping out at 0.2195. The H₁ column is not: 1.2373 against 0.5997 is a factor of 2.06, and the null reaches 0.5012 at its worst seed. The harder question gets the cleaner answer, because a torus has one void and two loops, and the two loops have to be picked out of a crowd of sampling artefacts that the void has no analogue of. Multiplicity, not dimension, is what makes H₁ hard here.

The methods sentence has to carry every convention lab-02 established is not determined by the words "Vietoris–Rips": Rips persistent homology in dimensions 0 to 2 over ℤ/2, filtration parameter equal to the simplex diameter — so a 2-simplex on a unit equilateral triangle enters at 1.0, not at its circumradius — computed with gtda.homology.VietorisRipsPersistence (giotto-tda 0.6.2), edge collapse enabled, no maximum edge length.

Self-check 1. collapse_edges=True is not a default. How should it be reported?

Reported, with its kind named. Edge collapse replaces the complex with a smaller one having the same persistent homology, so it genuinely cannot change the barcode — the third option is wrong about the consequence even though it is right that the complex changes. But a reader encountering collapse_edges=True in your methods has no way to know that unless you say so, and the alternative — omitting it — leaves them unable to reproduce your runtime or to rule out that it mattered. This is lab-01's rule for library defaults, extended: report every non-default argument, and say for each whether it is performance or modelling.
Faded — what the null does and does not rule out.

The null's marginals are not merely similar to the data's; they are identical, the same multiset of values in each coordinate. So any difference between the two diagrams is attributable to the joint structure and to nothing else. It is the geometric analogue of lab-07's label permutation: destroy exactly the thing under test, preserve exactly everything else.

It is not the strongest null available. Something survives independent coordinate permutation.

Segment 2 · 30 min

Four rules, four answers, and the one that works

The whole argument in one picture. Six H₁ bars. The dashed line at 0.5248 is lab-05's stability threshold at the measured δ = 0.1312; the line at 0.5012 is the longest bar any of five null permutations produced. Two arguments with nothing in common — a theorem about displacement, and a shuffle of the coordinates — land seven pixels apart, which is the arresting part of the picture.

It is also a trap, and the barcode springs it. Agreeing to within 5% on the threshold, the two rules disagree by two features: 4δ keeps three bars, the null keeps five. The difference is bars 4 and 5, at 0.5238 and 0.5233, which fall inside that seven-pixel window — six pixels past the null line and 0.3 of a pixel short of the stability line. Closeness of thresholds says nothing about agreement of answers when the barcode is dense exactly where the thresholds are.

That last margin is worth more than either count. A δ of 0.130950 rather than 0.131206 — one part in five hundred — moves the proved rule's answer from three bars to five. It is sound and correctly applied, and its answer is balanced on the fourth significant figure of a quantity real data supplies as an estimate. And neither three nor five is the answer, which is two: the only thing separating the two green bars from the rest is the gap between them, a factor of 2.06, and no theorem in this module says anything about it.

Had δ been read off the noise standard deviation, the first line would have sat at 0.12, off the left of every bar, and the picture would have shown a proved rule excluding nothing at all. That version of this figure was drawn, and it was wrong.

That ordering is worth sitting with: the error made the rule weaker and more comfortable at the same time — 0.12 contradicts no hoped-for conclusion, and it still carried the word "proved" while doing so.

Self-check 2. The stability rule, correctly applied with δ = 0.1312, admits three bars where the answer is two. Is that a defect in the theorem?

Sound, not complete — in the standard directions, which are worth stating exactly. Sound: everything the rule asserts is true. A bar longer than 4δ lies further than 2δ from the diagonal, so no matching of cost δ can send it to the diagonal, so it has a genuine partner in the clean sample's diagram. Not complete: not everything true is asserted — a real feature of the clean diagram shorter than 4δ is rejected.

Everything turns on which diagram soundness is about, and it is the clean sample's, not the torus's. The problem set computes that diagram: it has six long H₁ bars of its own — 1.2963, 1.2843, 0.5425, 0.5175, 0.5161, 0.5048 — and every one of the six noisy bars has a partner there within 0.0488, well inside δ = 0.1312. So all six bars are real in the only sense the theorem knows about, and the rule admitting three is if anything conservative. The torus, meanwhile, has b₁ = 2. The four extra bars belong to the clean 600-point sample and not to the surface it came from: 600 points do not fill a torus, and sampling is not a displacement of the points, so the theorem was never asked about it. The rule is not weak or misapplied. It controls a different error.

The third option is the interesting trap, and it is more tempting now than it was with a threshold of 0.12: substituting a "sampling scale" for δ would make the rule bite and give the right answer. It would also no longer be the theorem — δ in Corollary 3.6 bounds how far the data moved, not how densely it was sampled, and swapping one for the other silently converts a proved statement into a heuristic wearing its clothes. Getting the right answer by that route is worse than getting three by the honest one.
Worked — why the null does not supply a threshold either.

Permuting three coordinates independently leaves a cloud filling roughly the same box at roughly the same density. Six hundred points scattered in a box have a Rips filtration with plenty of medium-lived 1-cycles: at intermediate scales the complex is neither disconnected nor filled in, and the gaps make loops.

How long is the longest such accident? It depends which permutation you drew. Five pre-specified seeds give 0.4192, 0.4584, 0.4442, 0.4006 and 0.5012 — a spread of 0.10, or 25% of the value, on a quantity being used as a cutoff. A threshold taken from one draw is a threshold calibrated on a single sample of a random variable, which is structurally the error lab-07 spent a segment on. Take the maximum over the five: 0.5012.

A null answers a yes/no question much better than it supplies a number. "Are bars 1 and 2 distinguishable from structureless data?" — 1.2965 and 1.2373 against a null maximum of at most 0.5012 at any seed, decisively yes, and the answer does not depend on which seed. "Where is the cut?" — the null cannot say, because it is built by destroying the joint structure while this cloud's artefacts come from sparse sampling of a surface. Two different mechanisms, so no reason the first one's upper tail marks the second one's boundary. Rescaling by the null (2.59, 2.47, 1.20 for the top three) reproduces the gap rule's separation rather than supplying independent evidence: dividing every number by the same constant does not create information — and the constant itself wobbles by 25%.
Guided — the gap rule at four seeds, and what that is evidence for.

At n = 400, four independent samples give leading H₁ lifetimes with gaps at position 2 every time, with ratios 1.41, 1.58, 1.89 and 1.58 — against 2.06 at n = 600. Before reading on: write down (i) what going from 600 points to 400 did to those ratios and why, (ii) what you predict at n = 200 with a reason rather than a guess, and (iii) what the four-for-four result is actually evidence about.
The three answers (i) Fewer points means a sparser sample, so the artefact loops live at larger scales and the third bar rises — 0.5997 at n = 600 becomes 0.6385 to 0.806 at n = 400 — while the two real bars barely move, because they are fixed by R and r rather than by the sampling. The gap therefore shrinks, from 2.06 to around 1.5.

(ii) At n = 200 the artefact scale grows further, roughly like the typical nearest-neighbour distance, so the gap closes towards 1. At some sample size the rule picks the wrong k — and it does so with no signal that it has, which is the property that makes a heuristic dangerous rather than merely approximate.

(iii) It is evidence about this generator far more than about the rule. A torus with R = 2 and r = 1 has two loops both large relative to the sampling scale; that is what lifts them above the crowd. Nothing here suggests the rule works on a shape whose features are closer together — and lab-01's original question, "what would you conclude if the ratio were 1.4?", is exactly the case these four seeds do not cover. At seed 1 the ratio is 1.41.

Segment 3 · 25 min

The report, and what leaves the laboratory

Theorem 13.1 (Dey and Wang, printed 393, established in lab-06): for persistence diagrams D and D′, Λ∞(D, D′) ≤ dB(D, D′) — the landscape map is 1-Lipschitz.

Corollary 3.6 (Oudot, printed 61, established in lab-05): for q-tame f, g on X, db(dgm f, dgm g) ≤ ‖f − g‖∞.

Cited to their own books and by their own numbers, because the two number differently and this module's rule is never to carry a theorem number across books.

The features here are persistence landscapes on a 100-point grid. Twice the first three H₁ layer peaks are 1.2896, 1.2296 and 0.5975, against lifetimes of 1.2965, 1.2373 and 0.5997 — agreement to about 0.5%, and not exact, for two reasons: the grid samples the landscape between its peaks (lab-06), and layer k is the k-th upper envelope rather than the k-th tent, which coincide only for nested bars.

Self-check 3. What do these landscape numbers inherit from Theorem 13.1?

Two of three clauses. (i) Diagram to landscape function: inherits Theorem 13.1 exactly. (ii) Landscape function to the sampled array: does not — lab-06 measured a ratio of 1.0684 where the theorem permits 1, with an excess of the order of the grid spacing. (iii) Array to peak height: a maximum is 1-Lipschitz, so this is safe. The honest claim therefore names the grid: stable up to the discretisation error at n_bins = 100. The fourth option confuses this with lab-06's Theorem 13.3, which is the persistence image's, and is in 1-Wasserstein.

Self-check 4. Which of this unit's methods transfers unchanged to real data, where the answer is unknown and δ is not written into a generator?

The null. It is constructed from the data itself, needs no known answer, no noise model and no second sample, and it still rules something out. The first option fails because there is one sample — a bootstrap or subsampling sweep is its nearest substitute and is a different, weaker thing. The third is half-right and the missing half is fatal: all four thresholds are computable, but what does not transfer is the knowledge of which one was right, and that is the entire content of Segment 2. The fourth is the trap the whole module has been circling: Corollary 3.6 does hold regardless, and it is useless without a δ, which real data does not supply.

Self-check 5. What must a disclosure paragraph contain that a conclusion paragraph must not?

The provenance of each conclusion. A conclusion says what was found; a disclosure says on what it rests — and the load-bearing sentence in this unit's disclosure is that the count of two was determined by a gap heuristic with no guarantee, while the two thresholds that do have guarantees both admit six. A reader who has that sentence can decide what to believe; a reader who has only "we found b₁ = 2, consistent with a torus" cannot. The fourth option names something that belongs there — discarded analyses are part of the record — but "and nothing else" makes it wrong, and it is the smaller half.
