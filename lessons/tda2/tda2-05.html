Discrete Morse theory and graph reconstruction (tda2-05)

Morse theory with no calculus at all: pair each cell with a neighbour, and whatever is left unpaired is critical.

Smooth Morse theory needs a manifold, a smooth function, non-degenerate critical points and a gradient flow. Forman's observation is that almost none of that machinery was load-bearing. What actually did the work was the flow's ability to cancel cells in pairs, and a purely combinatorial pairing of each simplex with a neighbouring one reproduces it.

The payoff is not aesthetic. A complex with a million simplices can carry a Morse vector field with a handful of critical cells, and those critical cells compute the same homology. That is a preprocessing step that runs before tda1-09's reduction ever starts — and, run in reverse with persistence choosing what to cancel, it becomes an algorithm for pulling road networks out of GPS traces.

Prediction gate — commit before reading on. A discrete Morse vector field pairs simplices with neighbours and leaves the rest critical. Suppose someone hands you a pairing in which some closed chain of arrows runs round a loop and returns to its start. Predict what breaks.

Correct. Acyclicity is not a technical convenience — it is the entire content. Along a V-path the function values on the facets of matched pairs strictly decrease, so a cyclic path would require a value to be strictly less than itself. That is why Fact 10.1 holds, and why a matching is only called a Morse matching when it is acyclic. Drop it and Fact 10.2 fails, so there is no Morse function behind the pairing, and Ghrist's Theorem 7.23 — that Morse homology equals cellular homology — has no discrete gradient field to apply to.

Why this matters for the mission: A discrete Morse vector field pairs cells so the unpaired critical cells generate a much smaller complex with the same homology — a preprocessing step that shrinks a filtration before tda1-09's reduction ever runs. Persistence-guided cancellation then reconstructs graphs, such as road networks and neuron traces, from noisy density fields.

Segment 1 · ~25 min

Morse functions, matchings, and what the critical cells compute

Definition 10.1 (discrete Morse function). A function f : K → ℝ on a simplicial complex K is a discrete Morse function if for every p-simplex σp:

#{σp−1 : σp−1 is a facet of σp and f(σp−1) ≥ f(σp)} ≤ 1, and
#{σp+1 : σp+1 is a coface of σp and f(σp+1) ≤ f(σp)} ≤ 1.

— Dey & Wang §10.1, Definition 10.1, p. 290

In words: f is supposed to increase with dimension, and it is allowed to fail in at most one direction downwards and at most one direction upwards at each simplex. Dey and Wang note that by a result of Chari the two conditions force the two sets to be disjoint — a simplex cannot be the exceptional facet of one pair and the exceptional cofacet of another. That disjointness is exactly what makes the induced pairing well defined.

Definition 10.2 (matching). A set of ordered pairs M = {(σ, τ)} is a matching in K if σ is a facet of τ in every pair, and any simplex appears in at most one pair. Such an M defines disjoint subsets L, U ⊆ K with a bijection μ : L → U. — Dey & Wang §10.1, Definition 10.2, p. 290

Definition 10.3 (V-path and Morse matching). Given a matching M and k > 0, a V-path is a sequence π : σ0 ≺ σ1 ≺ ⋯ ≺ σk where for 0 < i < k, σi ≠ μ(σi−1) implies σi+1 = μ(σi) — an alternating sequence of facets and cofacets whose consecutive pairs alternate between matched and unmatched. It is cyclic if σ0 is a facet of σk or σ0 = μ(σk); M is cyclic if it contains such a path and acyclic otherwise. An acyclic matching is a Morse matching. — Dey & Wang §10.1, Definition 10.3, pp. 291–292

Fact 10.1. The matching induced by a Morse function on K is acyclic, thus is a Morse matching. — Dey & Wang §10.1, Fact 10.1, p. 291

Why. Dey and Wang's observation is that along a sequence like a V-path, the function values on the facets of the matched pairs strictly decrease. A cyclic path returns to its own starting simplex, which would force that value to be strictly less than itself. So no cyclic path can exist. ∎

Fact 10.2. A Morse matching M in K defines a Morse function on K. — Dey & Wang §10.1, Fact 10.2, p. 291

Facts 10.1 and 10.2 together are the reason the subject is usable. The two notions determine each other, so one may define the theory with functions and compute with matchings — and matchings are finite combinatorial data, which functions are not.

Definition 10.4 (discrete Morse vector field). A DMVF V in K is a partition V = C ⊔ L ⊔ U where L is the set of facets paired with a unique cofacet in U under a Morse matching M with μ(L) = U, and C is the set of unpaired simplices, called critical simplices. Each pair (σ, μ(σ)) is drawn as an arrow with tail in σ and head in μ(σ); the critical simplices are the critical points of the field. — Dey & Wang §10.1.1, Definition 10.4, p. 292

Proposition 10.1 (Morse inequalities). For a Morse function f on K with induced Morse matching M, writing ci for the number of critical i-simplices and βi for the Betti numbers:

(a) weak: (i) ci ≥ βi for all i ≥ 0; (ii) cp − cp−1 + ⋯ ± c0 = βp − βp−1 + ⋯ ± β0 where K is p-dimensional.
(b) strong: ci − ci−1 + ⋯ ± c0 ≥ βi − βi−1 + ⋯ ± β0 for all i ≥ 0.

— Dey & Wang §10.1, Proposition 10.1, p. 293

Note the asymmetry between (a)(i) and (a)(ii). The first is an inequality — critical cells are at least as numerous as the homology they must generate. The second is an equality, because both alternating sums compute the Euler characteristic, a topological invariant that cannot change when cells are cancelled in pairs of adjacent dimension.

Count it on the acyclic state above, and count every gold cell. The matched pairs are (e01, T0), (e23, T1), (v1, e13) and (v2, e02), so the unpaired simplices are v0, v3 and the diagonal e03: c0 = 2, c1 = 1, c2 = 0. Then (a)(ii) reads c0 − c1 + c2 = 2 − 1 + 0 = 1, which is the Euler characteristic of the disk, and β0 − β1 + β2 = 1 − 0 + 0 = 1 agrees. Missing the critical edge and counting only the two gold vertices would give 2, and the equality would appear to fail — the commonest way to misread a picture like this one.

Ghrist's Theorem 7.23. For any discrete gradient field V, MH•(V) ≅ Hcell•(X). — Ghrist §7.8, Theorem 7.23, p. 150

This is strictly stronger than the inequalities. Proposition 10.1 bounds the counts; Theorem 7.23 says the small complex built on the critical cells computes the homology exactly. Ghrist's framing is worth having alongside Dey and Wang's: a discrete vector field partitions the cells into pairs (σα ◁ τα) with σα a codimension-1 face, the leftover cells are Cr(V), a discrete flowline is a chain of such pairs, and a discrete gradient field is one devoid of periodic flowlines. Lifting to the chain complex, V induces a map Ck → Ck+1 and the flow is

Φ := Id + ∂V + V∂.

The property that makes all of this computational is that, unlike a continuous-time flow, the discrete flow terminates: Φ∞ = limn→∞Φn is constant for n sufficiently large. A limit reached in finitely many steps can be computed rather than approximated.

Self-check 1. Why is part (a)(ii) of Proposition 10.1 an equality rather than an inequality?

Cancelling a pair removes one i-cell and one (i−1)-cell, which changes the alternating sum by (+1) + (−1) = 0. So the alternating count is invariant under the whole cancellation process, and at the two extremes — all cells critical, and a minimal field — it must give the same number. That number is the Euler characteristic, computable either from cells or from Betti numbers.

Self-check 2. What is the practical content of Facts 10.1 and 10.2 taken together?

The pair of facts is an equivalence of two descriptions, and the value is that one description is computable. The second option is false and is precisely what the Morse inequalities fail to promise — ci ≥ βi is a bound, and achieving equality is a hard optimisation problem in general. The third is a misreading of Fact 10.1: acyclicity is automatic for a matching that came from a Morse function, not for an arbitrary matching, which is exactly what the prediction gate was about.

Segment 2 · ~25 min

Cancellation, and pulling a graph out of a density field

Start from the trivial DMVF in which every simplex is critical — the field induced by f(σ) = dim σ, whose matching is empty. Then reduce the number of critical cells by cancelling them in pairs.

Definition 10.5 (critical V-path). Given a DMVF V = C ⊔ L ⊔ U induced by M, a V-path π : σ0 ≺ ⋯ ≺ σk is critical if both σ0 and σk are critical. — Dey & Wang §10.1.1, Definition 10.5, p. 293

Definition 10.6 (cancellation). Let (σ0, σk) be a pair of critical simplices joined by a critical V-path π. The pair is cancelled by modifying the matching along π — shifting the matched pairs so that σ0 and σk become paired and the interior simplices re-pair accordingly. — Dey & Wang §10.1.1, Definition 10.6, pp. 293–294

Proposition 10.2. Given a Morse matching M, suppose we cancel a pair of critical simplices σ and σ′ in a DMVF V via a critical V-path to obtain a new matching M′. Then M′ remains a Morse matching if and only if this V-path is the only critical V-path connecting σ and σ′ in V. — Dey & Wang §10.2, Proposition 10.2, p. 296

The uniqueness hypothesis is doing real work, and the failure mode is easy to see. Cancelling reverses the arrows along one path. If a second critical V-path already ran between the same two simplices, it now runs the opposite way to the reversed one, and the two together close a loop — a cyclic V-path. By Definition 10.3 the matching is then no longer a Morse matching, and everything in Segment 1 stops applying.

Which pairs to cancel: persistence decides

Proposition 10.3. Let (v1,e1), …, (vn,en) be the sequence of all non-essential persistence pairs of vertices and edges, sorted in increasing order of the appearance of the edges ei in a filtration of a 1-complex K. Let V0 be the DMVF in K with all simplices critical. Then the cancellations may be performed in that order. — Dey & Wang §10.2.1, Proposition 10.3, pp. 295–296

Persistence pairs are the natural cancellation order because a persistence pair is exactly a creator paired with its destroyer — which is the relation a critical V-path realises. The persistence computation hands over a ready-made list of cancellable pairs, ordered by significance, and a threshold δ on persistence becomes a dial controlling how much gets cancelled.

Theorem 10.5. Let K be a finite simplicial 2-complex whose underlying space is a 2-manifold without boundary, and let 𝓕 be a simplex-wise filtration of K. Starting from the trivial DMVF where each simplex is critical, one can obtain a DMVF in K by cancelling the vertex–edge and edge–triangle persistence pairs given by 𝓕. — Dey & Wang §10.2.1, Theorem 10.5, p. 297

Theorem 10.6. Given a simplicial 1-complex or a simplicial 2-manifold K with n simplices, one can compute (a) a DMVF by cancelling all persistence pairs resulting from a given filtration of K in O(nα(n)) time; (b) a DMVF as above when the filtration is induced by a given PL-function on K in O(n log n) time. — Dey & Wang §10.2.2, Theorem 10.6, p. 300

Note the hypotheses in Theorem 10.5. It is a statement about 2-manifolds without boundary, extended by duality to filtrations of a simplicial d-complex in which each (d−1)-simplex has at most two d-simplices as cofacets. It is not a statement about arbitrary complexes, and the reason is Proposition 10.2: uniqueness of the critical V-path is what a manifold's local structure supplies.

Worked example 1: reconstructing a graph

The problem. A density field ρ over a region Ω — GPS traces over a city, or an image of neuron fluorescence — is concentrated near an unknown embedded graph G. Recover G.

Step 1 — the noise model must be stated first. Definition 10.7: ρ is a (β, ν, ω)-approximation of a connected graph G if (C-1) there is an ω-neighbourhood Gω of G that deformation retracts to G; and (C-2) ρ(x) ∈ [β, β+ν] for x ∈ Gω and ρ(x) ∈ [0, ν] otherwise, with β > 2ν.

Step 2 — read the conditions. (C-1) says the neighbourhood has the same topology as the hidden graph — without it there is no sense in which recovering Gω recovers G. (C-2) says the density concentrates: significantly higher inside than outside, with only small fluctuation on either side. The separation β > 2ν is what makes "significantly" precise; Dey and Wang's illustration is an ideal field fG equal to β on Gω and 0 elsewhere, plus a perturbation g bounded in [0, ν].

Step 3 — the algorithm. Build a DMVF on a triangulation of Ω by cancelling persistence pairs, keep the critical edges whose persistence exceeds a threshold δ, and take the union of their 1-unstable manifolds — the descending paths traced out by the vector field. Theorem 10.9: the time complexity of MORSERECON is O(Pert(K)), where Pert(K) is the time to compute persistence pairings for K. The reconstruction is free relative to the persistence computation it consumes.

Step 4 — what is guaranteed. Theorem 10.12: under the input assumption, the output satisfies Ĝ ⊆ Gω. Proposition 10.13: under the same assumption, Ĝ is homotopy equivalent to G. And Theorem 10.14: for any deformation retraction H : Gω × [0,1] → Gω with H(Gω,1) = G, the restriction H|Ĝ is a homotopy from the embedded Ĝ to some G′ ⊆ G, where G and G′ have the same first Betti number.

Step 5 — what is not. None of these says Ĝ = G. The output lies within ω of the truth and has the right topology; it is not claimed to be metrically accurate beyond ω, and individual output edges are not claimed to correspond to individual true edges. §10.5 applies exactly this to road networks and neuron networks, which is where the distinction matters.

Worked example 2 (faded): why cancellation cannot be done greedily. Two critical simplices σ and σ′ are joined by two distinct critical V-paths.

What cancellation does. Cancelling along one path reverses the matched pairs along it, so that arrows which pointed one way now point ⟨your step⟩.

The collision. The second critical V-path still runs between σ and σ′ in its original direction. Following the reversed first path out and the untouched second path back produces a V-path from σ0 to itself — that is, a ⟨your step: what kind of path?⟩.

The consequence. By Definition 10.3 the matching is now cyclic, so it is not a Morse matching; Fact 10.2 no longer supplies a Morse function, and the critical cells no longer compute the homology. This is exactly the content of Proposition 10.2's "if and only if".

Gap solutions: the arrows now point the other way; and the composite is a cyclic V-path.

The pattern to carry out of this unit. Every guarantee in §10.4 is conditional on the input assumption — the (β, ν, ω) noise model. That model is a hypothesis about the data, not a property the algorithm can establish, and MORSERECON returns an answer whether or not it holds. The checkable part is the model; the claim licensed is containment in Gω plus homotopy equivalence; and the parameter δ that decides which critical edges survive is a choice the analyst makes and must report.

Self-check 3. Why does Theorem 10.5 assume the underlying space is a 2-manifold without boundary?

Cancellation is legitimate exactly when the critical V-path is unique, and what supplies that uniqueness is the local structure — Dey and Wang extend the theorem by duality to complexes in which each (d−1)-simplex has at most two d-simplices as cofacets, which is precisely the manifold condition made combinatorial. Persistence pairs are defined for any filtration, and the Morse inequalities hold for any complex.

Self-check 4. MORSERECON is run and returns a graph Ĝ. What has been proved?

Theorem 10.12 gives containment in the ω-neighbourhood and Proposition 10.13 gives homotopy equivalence, with Theorem 10.14 exhibiting the homotopy explicitly and matching first Betti numbers. Equality is never claimed and would be the wrong thing to want from a density field. The third option overcorrects: the model is a hypothesis about the data, and hypotheses about data can be argued for from the acquisition process even when they cannot be certified.

Self-check 5. Theorem 10.9 gives complexity O(Pert(K)). What is the practical reading?

Expressing complexity relative to Pert(K) is a deliberate statement about where the cost lives: whatever the persistence algorithm costs, the reconstruction adds only lower-order work. It is not a claim of linearity — persistence itself is not linear in general — and it certainly does not imply repeated recomputation, which would defeat the point.

Fact 10.1. Let f be a discrete Morse function on K and M the matching it induces, so that (σ, τ) ∈ M exactly when σ is the unique facet of τ with f(σ) ≥ f(τ), equivalently τ the unique cofacet of σ with f(τ) ≤ f(σ). Consider a V-path π : σ0 ≺ σ1 ≺ ⋯ ≺ σk and look at the facets of its matched pairs — the terms of even index, say. Dey and Wang's observation is that these values strictly decrease along π: at each step the path leaves a matched pair, where f does not increase, and enters an unmatched incidence, where by Definition 10.1's counting condition f must strictly decrease, since the exceptional facet has already been used by the match.

Now suppose π were cyclic, so that σ0 is a facet of σk or σ0 = μ(σk). Then the sequence of values returns to the value at σ0. But it strictly decreased at every step, so f(σ0) < f(σ0), a contradiction. Hence no cyclic V-path exists and M is acyclic — that is, a Morse matching. ∎

Invariance of the alternating sum. A cancellation as in Definition 10.6 removes exactly one critical simplex of dimension p−1 and one of dimension p from C, since σ0 and σk become paired with each other and the interior simplices merely re-pair among themselves — they were already in L ⊔ U and remain there. So cp−1 drops by 1 and cp drops by 1, and the alternating sum changes by

−(−1)p−1 − (−1)p = (−1)p − (−1)p = 0.

The two contributions have opposite signs precisely because the dimensions are adjacent. Hence Σi(−1)ici is unchanged by any single cancellation, and by induction by any finite sequence of them. ∎

Consistency with Proposition 10.1(a)(ii). Begin at the trivial DMVF, where every simplex is critical and the alternating sum is Σi(−1)i(number of i-simplices) — the Euler characteristic computed from the cells. Cancellation preserves that number, so every DMVF obtained by cancelling has the same alternating sum. Proposition 10.1(a)(ii) identifies it with the alternating sum of Betti numbers, so the identity holds at every stage of the process and no sequence of cancellations can contradict it. ∎

What this does and does not license. Invariance of the alternating sum is a conservation law, not an optimality statement: it says cancellation cannot cheat the Euler characteristic, but says nothing about how far ci can be driven towards βi. That gap — between what the inequalities permit and what an algorithm achieves — is exactly what persistence-guided cancellation is trying to close, and Theorem 10.5's hypotheses are the price of a guarantee that it does.
