Branch `s1-aa`, off `main` at d6d1bef. Thirty-one units against Aluffi,
*Algebra: Notes from the Underground*, with Carter's *Visual Group Theory*
carrying the optional on-ramp and joining for three of the group units.

Two units existed before this branch and had never been checked against
either book; twenty-nine are new. This is the largest module in S1 and the
last, and it is the first one that is not single-book — which is what exposed
the three gate defects in §3.

## 2. Source boundary

### 2.1 Folios

**Aluffi**: a single offset of **−18** over the whole main text. Read off the
page at PDF 22 (printed 4), 23 (5), 24 (6), 25 (7) and 26 (8). Front matter
is roman and sits at −2 (PDF 12 is x, PDF 17 is xv); the main text opens at
PDF 19, "Part I / Rings", which is printed 1.

**Carter**: a single offset of **−15**. Read off the page at PDF 21 (printed
6), 22 (7) and 23 (8).

Neither book has a second document bound in behind it, so neither has
Abbott's twice-existing printed page. Both were regenerated with `--refresh`
and both report zero dropped plateaus.

### 2.2 Chapter openers carry their first section, and carry no folio

Aluffi opens every chapter with the chapter title and the first section
heading on the same page — `# 1 The Integers` / `## 1.1 The Well-Ordering
Principle and Induction`, PDF 21 — and suppresses the folio there. That is
why the index reports **zero chapter gaps** for both books: there is no page
from which no section is in force. Abbott and Axler both have such pages and
keep their gap rows; this is a difference between books, not a relaxation.

The suppressed folio at a chapter opener is harmless in the middle of the
book, where the fitted plateau covers the page anyway. At the **lower edge of
the first plateau** it is not, and §3 records what it cost.

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 F-1 — the two pre-existing units, first checked here

Five repairs across `aa-00` and `aa-01`, every one of them a first check:

| where | was | is |
|---|---|---|
| aa-00 set, Sources | Carter Exercises 1.1–1.7, pp. 4–7 | pp. 4–8 |
| aa-00 set, Q4 | Exercise 1.6, p. 7 | p. 8 |
| aa-00 lesson | §1.3–1.4, pp. 4–7 | §1.2–1.4 |
| aa-00 lesson | mission strip from another unit | quoted verbatim |
| aa-01 lesson | mission strip embellished | quoted verbatim |

Carter's Exercises 1.1–1.4 are on printed 7 and 1.5–1.9 on printed 8: PDF 23
heads `8  1. What is a group?` and carries Exercise 1.5 onward. The lesson's
§1.3–1.4 was corrected to §1.2–1.4 to match the unit's own Sources line,
which was already right.

Both mission strips had been rewritten rather than quoted. aa-00 carried a
sentence belonging to another unit entirely ("Groups measure symmetry;
homology groups measure holes"); aa-01 promoted the syllabus's `Z` to `ℤ` and
added a clause about integer coefficients. Gate 8 says verbatim. It is
allowed to be the more attractive sentence and still be the wrong one.

One further repair in aa-00's set is not a folio error: it cited "gt-02's
Definition 10.12" for a contrast remark — a forward reference to a unit the
student has not reached and this lesson does not teach, which the coverage
gate correctly reported as an uncovered result. The pointer to gt-02 stays;
the number goes.

### 3.2 F-2 — the primary book was a property of the run, not of the unit

`citations.py` read the primary book once, from `paths[0]`, and used it for
every path behind it. A run spanning two books therefore measured the second
book's folios against the first book's pagination.

It was silent, and worse than silent: a wrong pagination still resolves to
real pages, and some of those pages still carry the result named. Running

```
python scripts/citations.py lessons/aa/aa-00.html lessons/aa/aa-01.html ...
```

checked aa-01 — which cites Aluffi throughout — against **Carter**, because
aa-00 came first on the command line, and reported `PASS 5, FAIL 3`. The
three failures were not aa-01's errors and the five passes were not aa-01's
citations being right.

Every module before this one was single-book, so the defect had nothing to
show. It is fixed by resolving the book per path from the unit's own syllabus
line; `--book` still overrides the whole run, which is what it is for. The
test for it was **watched to fail** against the old code before it was kept.

### 3.3 F-3 — the two citation gates disagreed about the same file

`check_sections.py` began attribution at None and waited to be told a book.
That is right for the first gate a citation meets and wrong for the second,
because the two then disagree: aa-01's thirteen `Aluffi §1.1, p. 5` citations
**passed** `citations.py` — bare "Aluffi" matches neither Aluffi key, so the
clause stays with the unit's primary — and were **skipped whole** by
`check_sections.py` as naming no indexed book.

One gate saying PASS and the other saying nothing at all, about the same
thirteen citations. Reading the primary from the same syllabus line makes the
two agree by construction.

### 3.4 F-4 — a section range was read as its first label

`§1.2–1.5, pp. 4–7` was parsed as the single label `1.2`, and every page in
the clause was tested against it. Printed 7 is §1.5, so a correct citation
failed. Four of aa-00's five reported failures were this, with nothing wrong
with any of them.

A range now names every section between its ends. The run is read **off the
index**, not counted out: sections do not always run 1, 2, 3 — a book may
stop at §3.3 and go to §4.1 — and arithmetic expansion would admit sections
that do not exist. A range naming an end the book does not have is left as-is
and fails loudly rather than resolving to something plausible.

### 3.5 F-5 — a heading on the first page of the book was dropped in silence

`fit_offsets` skips a page whose folio nothing could read, which is right in
the middle of a plateau — the surrounding range still covers it. At the
**lower edge** of the first plateau it is not covered by anything: `printed()`
answers None, the page is skipped, and any section heading standing there is
dropped from the index without a word.

Aluffi's chapter-1 opening page is exactly that page. **§1.1 was missing from
a 76-section index**, and the consequence was the reverse of the usual one:
the gate did not stay quiet, it made the *correct* citation `§1.1, p. 3` the
thing that failed. The index reported 75 sections and named no fewer books
than it should, so nothing about the output said anything was missing.

The first plateau now reaches down over folio-less pages while the offset
keeps the printed page at 1 or above. Both halves matter: a book's main text
starts at printed 1, so the guard stops the walk exactly where the book does,
and roman-numbered front matter — which also reads as folio-less — is not
swallowed and handed printed page 0 and below.

A blank run **between** two plateaus has no such pin. Either neighbour's
offset could be the right one and the page tree does not say which, so those
pages are reported by `--refresh` rather than guessed at.

## 4. What the section index caught the moment it existed

Carter had no index before this branch, so `check_sections.py` had never
looked at aa-00 at all: 28 citations, reported as unchecked in the NOTE line
and checked by nothing. Indexing Carter turned them into five FAILs the same
minute, four of them F-4 and one of them real.

That is the argument for indexing a book before authoring against it rather
than after. Across the corpus the same NOTE still reports 1585 citations
naming eight unindexed books, which is the honest denominator for the PASS
line and the queue for whoever indexes next.

## 5. Authoring rules carried into this module

The five rules the `an` module cost are in
`docs/plans/2026-08-23-s1-an-content.md` §6 and apply unchanged. Two are
worth restating because this module is where they bite hardest:

1. **Never carry a result number across books.** Aluffi and Carter both
   number `Definition 1.1`, `Theorem 1.3`, `Exercise 1.6`, and both are in
   scope in the same module. aa-01's citations resolved against the wrong one
   of the two and five of them passed.
2. **Assert nothing the set asks the student to prove.** The step a problem
   turns on is the step to write out, not the step to call routine.

One rule is new here:

3. **Name the book, not the author, when the author has two books in the
   bookmap.** "Aluffi" matches neither `Aluffi Underground` nor `Aluffi
   Chapter 0`, by design — the most specific match wins and a bare surname
   has no specificity to offer. Citations that say only "Aluffi" survive on
   the unit's primary book, which is correct but is a fallback, and it stops
   being correct in any unit that cites both. Write **"Aluffi Underground"**
   in a Sources line, where the disambiguation belongs; a `<span class="cite">`
   inside the prose may keep the short form.
