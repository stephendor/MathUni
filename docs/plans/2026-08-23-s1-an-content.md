## 2. Source boundary

### 2.1 Abbott's folio, and a second document inside the same file

Abbott's page tree fits a **single offset of −12 over PDF 13–269**, giving
printed 1–257 — no drifting plateaus, unlike Axler.

But the PDF continues. **PDF 277–429 is the Instructor's Solutions Manual,
bound in behind the book and restarting at printed 1.** Printed page 100
therefore exists twice in one file, and `citations.py` reports both candidates
for every folio it resolves: `Exercise 1.3.4 is not on printed p. 14 (PDF 26,
290)`.

Two consequences, and they pull in opposite directions.

For **citation checking**, `citations.py` already documents the situation and
accepts a hit on either candidate. That is the right call there — picking one
and being confidently wrong is worse — but it does weaken the gate for this
book specifically, because the solutions manual restates every exercise number.
A citation naming the wrong page could in principle pass because the
corresponding manual page happens to restate that exercise. No instance of this
was observed; the fourteen wrong-page citations in an-01's set all failed
loudly. Recorded here so that the next author knows the gate is softer on
Abbott than on Axler.

For the **section index**, the same tolerance would be a defect rather than a
concession: the manual's headings are not this book's sections, and indexing
them would move section boundaries onto pages the book does not have.
`check_sections.py --refresh` now drops a plateau whose printed range repeats
one already accepted, and says so. Axler's three ascending plateaus are the
negative control and are all kept.

**Every folio in this module was read off the main-text page — PDF = printed +
12 — and never off the manual.**

## 3. Findings

### 3.1 F-1 — twenty-seven wrong folios in the three pre-existing units

an-01, an-02 and an-03 were written before this branch and had never been run
through `citations.py` with the book drive present. All 91 of their **section
labels** were correct; 27 of their **page numbers** were not.

The pattern is uniform and worth naming, because it is what an author does when
writing from a table of contents rather than from the page: exercises were cited
at the page where their *section* starts rather than where the exercise list
actually is. Exercise 1.3.6 was cited at 17 and is on 18; Exercise 1.4.4 was
cited at 20 and is on 27; Exercises 1.5.6–1.5.8 were cited at 30–31 and are on
32. Three Sources lines gave page *ranges* whose endpoints did not cover the
results named inside them, which is the same error at span scale — a range is
read as its two endpoints, so `pp. 18–22` does not contain printed 21.

One error was worse than a page. an-01's guided proof cited **Lemma 1.3.8**,
and there is no Lemma 1.3.8 in this book. The ε-characterisation of the
supremum, which is what that proof proves, is **Lemma 1.3.7 on printed 16**.
The number 1.3.8 belongs to an exercise. A wrong result number that happens to
name a real result of a different kind is exactly the failure `citations.py`'s
`KINDS` handling exists for, and here it caught it.

All 27 repaired; the three units now check 54 lesson citations and 74 problem
set citations with none wrong.

### 3.2 F-2 — an-10's hook promises a result from §4.4

The syllabus hook for an-10 is *"Continuity recast: preimages of open sets are
open. Remember pw-03?"*, and an-10's declared resource is Abbott 4.2–4.3. The
topological characterisation of continuity is **Exercise 4.4.11, printed 120** —
in §4.4, which is an-11's resource.

Handled as la-15's F-1 was: the statement is given in the lesson with its
justification, cited to the section it actually comes from, and the boundary is
recorded in the lesson's own footer rather than crossed silently. an-10 uses
§4.4 for nothing else. No syllabus change is proposed; the hook is accurate
about the mathematics and only the section pin is narrow.

### 3.3 F-3 — an-14 is the module's first two-book unit

an-14's declared resources are `Abbott 8.2 (or equiv.)` and `Lindstrom ch. 3`,
and the lesson cites both: Abbott for the definitions and the Baire material,
Lindström for the treatment that states compactness and the open covering
property in a metric space from the start. `citations.py` splits the sources
correctly and checks 44 citations across the two books.

One practical note for the next author. The book must be named as
`resources/bookmap.json` spells it — **Lindström**, with an o-umlaut. A first
draft wrote *Lindstrøm* with an o-slash, and every Lindström citation was then
attributed to Abbott and reported as a wrong page. The failure is loud, but the
message is misleading: it names the wrong book rather than the wrong spelling.

### 3.4 F-4 — two citations named the right page and the wrong result

Both caught by hand, not by a gate, and both the same shape as the la-08 defect
recorded on the previous branch: the result number cited is not the one the
sentence is about, and it happens to sit on the page named, so the citation
gate passes.

- an-08's You-try cited **Exercise 3.3.5** (a list of six sets to classify) for
  the statement of **Exercise 3.3.4** (K compact and F closed gives K ∩ F
  compact). Both on printed 88.
- an-12's You-try cited **Exercise 5.3.1** for a statement about injectivity;
  5.3.1 is the Lipschitz exercise. The You-try was rewritten to Exercise
  5.3.1's actual content, which is a better exercise for the unit anyway.

The standing observation applies: a gate that verifies section, result and page
independently can pass an assertion whose parts do not belong together.

### D-1 — wire `check_sections.py` into CI now?

The `la` branch recommended waiting until more than one book was indexed, so
that the gate's first CI run had a meaningful denominator rather than a screen
of SKIPs. **Two books are now indexed** — Axler at 30 sections and Abbott at 51
— and between them they cover Semester 1's `pw`, `la` and `an` modules and part
of `an2`.

Evidence for wiring it now: it needs no book drive, so it runs anywhere; it
exits 2 rather than 0 when it cannot run; across this branch it checked 28
files with 0 wrong labels, and on the previous branch it found 22 wrong labels
in one lesson under construction and 2 in already-merged work. Evidence
against: the S2–S4 modules cite Hatcher, Munkres, Dey, Ghrist and others that
are not indexed, so a corpus-wide run would still be SKIP-heavy.

Recommendation: wire it in **scoped to the books that are indexed** — that is,
fail the build on a wrong label and report the SKIP count as a number rather
than as a wall of lines. Owner decision; nothing on this branch depends on it.

**Updated after review.** The argument above cited "28 files with 0 wrong
labels" as evidence the gate was working. §5.1 shows that number was vacuous
for Abbott: no numbered citation was being checked at all. That strengthens the
case for wiring it in rather than weakening it — a gate nobody runs in CI is a
gate whose silence nobody has reason to question, and this one stayed silent
through a full module review. The count-of-unchecked line added in §5.1 is what
makes the CI output readable: `PASS n files, 0 wrong` followed by `NOTE 195
citations named a book with no section index`, so the denominator is visible
without a wall of SKIPs.

## 5. Gate changes on this branch

Three, all in `scripts/check_sections.py`, and each one prompted by something
this module needed.

**Abbott's sections are numbered, Axler's are lettered.** The heading pattern
now has both shapes, and the shape is read off the book rather than configured.
That discriminator is not decoration: running the numbered pattern over Axler
invents sections out of *result* headings — it reported 10.58 and 10.59, which
are a Definition and a Theorem. The lettered pattern is tried over the whole
book first and wins outright if it matches anything.

**A bound-in second document is not indexed.** §2.1 above. `main_text_plateaus`
drops a plateau whose printed span repeats one already accepted, and `--refresh`
reports what it dropped.

**A page can carry two sections, and Abbott's mostly do.** The one-label rule
made `§1.4, Exercise 1.4.13, p. 29` a FAIL, and that citation is correct.
Section-start pages now admit the previous section's label as well — but only
where the book actually shares the page, which `--refresh` decides by looking
for body text above the heading. Abbott shares 38 of 51 starts; Axler shares
none, because Axler begins every section on a fresh page. That difference is
what keeps the relaxation honest: la-06's `§3.A, Definition 3.12, p. 59` — the
defect the gate was built for — still fires, and is the negative control in both
the selftest and the test file.

Also on this branch: the truncation fix from the `la` review was completed.
`unreadable()` reported NO VERDICT only when the retained prefix held no page at
all, so a citation naming a correct page *before* the cut and a wrong one after
it read as fully checked. Every capped citation is unreadable now.

  `check_sections` selftest 23 → 35, tests 22 → 30.

### 5.1 Two more, added in review — and one of them retracts a claim above

**The numbered half of the gate was never running.** `CITE` matched `§N` and
`§N.LETTER` only, so Abbott's `§1.4` parsed as the label `1`, `check_text` read
that as a bare chapter number and skipped it as a non-claim. Every numbered
citation in the repository passed without being looked at — **476 of them in
this module alone**, reported as `PASS 28 file(s) checked, 0 wrong label(s)`.
Adding the numbered *heading* pattern indexed the book; it did not check it, and
the two look identical from the outside. (Codex, PR #25.)

This retracts a sentence in §5's third paragraph as originally written, which
said the one-label rule "made `§1.4, Exercise 1.4.13, p. 29` a FAIL". It could
not have: that citation never reached `check_text`. The shared-page relaxation
is nevertheless load-bearing *now* — with `shared` removed, that citation fails
against §1.5 — so the change was right for a reason that was not yet true when
it was made. Both directions are in the selftest and the tests.

  Negative controls: `§9.9, p. 36` fails against a numbered index; `§1` is still
  not judged; la-06's `§3.A … p. 59` still fires.

**One file, several books.** Fixing `CITE` immediately produced 60 FAIL rows
across `pw-04` and `an2-*`, and not one of them was about a citation. The book
was chosen per FILE — the first indexed name appearing anywhere in the text —
so `pw-04`, which cites Abbott and Cummings in one Sources line, had its four
Cummings citations measured against Abbott's index, and the `an2` units had
Lindström's citations measured the same way.

This is the mechanism `citations.py` already removed; its `attribute()`
docstring names `pw-04` explicitly. `check_sections.py` had reintroduced it
behind a comment asserting that "every file in this repo cites one book", which
was never true. Attribution now reuses `citations.py`'s own `split_at_books` —
sticky, left to right, cut at each book name — rather than being re-derived,
because every hard case in that function (de-accenting `Lindström`, requiring a
consecutive phrase so `Cummings Real Analysis` beats `Cummings`) was a reported
failure before it was a rule.

A span belonging to a book with no section index is **counted and reported**,
not silently dropped: the run now ends with a line naming how many citations
were parsed and then not checked, and for which books. Corpus-wide that is 195
— Lindström 187, Dey 3, Cummings 2, Cummings Real Analysis 2 — and it is the
honest denominator for the `PASS` line above it.

  `check_sections` selftest 35 → 38, tests 30 → 39.

**Also fixed:** `heading = LETTERED if lettered >= 2 else NUMBERED` contradicted
the rule stated beside it, which is that the lettered pattern wins outright if
it finds *anything*. A book with a single lettered section fell through to
NUMBERED, matched nothing, and raised NoVerdict. (CodeRabbit, PR #25.)

## 6. Authoring rules this module cost

Each was learned by shipping the mistake and having a gate or a reader catch it.

1. **A page range is read as its endpoints.** `pp. 18–22` names 18 and 22 and
   nothing between. A Sources line covering results on 18, 21 and 27 must list
   `pp. 18, 21, 27`.
2. **A "Builds on" footer clause must not name the current unit's own results.**
   Pages attach per clause, so `whose Theorem 2.5.5 supplies the limit in
   Theorem 2.6.4's proof — §2.5, p. 56` checks 2.6.4 against page 56 and fails.
   Name the segment instead of the theorem.
3. **A footer clause naming a result with no page inherits the whole footer's
   pages.** an-14's last line named Theorem 3.2.3 with no folio and was checked
   against all seventeen pages the footer mentions. Give every such clause its
   own citation.
4. **Every numbered result a problem set cites must be named in its lesson.**
   Carried over from `la` and still the most frequent coverage failure — usually
   fixed by extending a "Builds on" line, which is where the cross-unit
   dependency belonged anyway.
5. **Name the book as the book map spells it.** F-3.

## 8. Review round on PR #25

Eleven findings from two reviewers; nine were real and are fixed, one named the
wrong file but was real in the file it should have named, and one did not
reproduce.

**Gate defects** — §5.1 above: the vacuous numbered-label parse (Codex, P1), the
per-file book attribution it exposed, and the `lettered >= 2` heading selection.

**Mathematics that was wrong or unproved:**

- **an-04 Problem 1.** The monotonicity argument derived a numerator whose sign
  needs `x_n ≥ 2 − √3 ≈ 0.2679`, but the proved invariant only gives `x_n >
  1/4`. Replaced with the monotone-map induction: `f(x) = 1/(4−x)` is increasing
  for `x < 4`, `x₂ ≤ x₁`, so `f` applied to both sides propagates the order. The
  failed route is kept and labelled, because the gap is instructive.
- **an-05 canvas.** `spread()` measured a fixed 60-term window, over which the
  harmonic partial sums spread by `≈ ln((N+61)/(N+1)) → 0`. The amber bar
  collapsed, and the picture taught that the harmonic partial sums are Cauchy —
  the exact opposite of the section. The horizon now grows with `N`: measured
  out to `e·N` the spread sits at `ln e = 1` for every `N` (verified: 0.98 at
  N=10, 1.00 at N=400) while the blue collapses to 0.
- **an-05 You-try.** "Two of the four are impossible" — only (c) is. (a) has
  `(−1)ⁿ/n`, (b) has `xₙ = n`, (d) has `0,1,0,2,0,3,…`. Corrected, with (d)
  called out as the one usually given up on. *(CodeRabbit filed this against
  `problems/sets/an-05.md`, which has 153 lines and no such text; it is in the
  lesson.)*
- **an-06 Problem 1.** The `p ≤ 0` case was disposed of in one clause claiming
  Theorem 2.4.6 "was never applicable because the terms are not decreasing".
  At `p = 0` the terms are constantly 1, which *is* decreasing in Abbott's sense
  — Definition 2.4.1 asks `bₙ ≥ bₙ₊₁`, verified on printed 51 — so the test
  applies and returns divergence. Split into `p < 0` (hypothesis fails) and
  `p = 0` (test applies, series diverges).
- **an-07 Problem 5(f).** The worked solution built `⋃ V_{ε2⁻ⁿ}(qₙ)` and then
  could not prove it omits anything, deferring to "total length" — Lebesgue
  measure, which this course has not built. Replaced with `ℝ \ {√2}`, which is
  open by part (e) of the same problem. The union is kept as a discussion of why
  a correct intuition resting on an unavailable theorem is not an argument.
- **an-08 canvas and Self-check 4.** Every band is `(x/2, 1)`, so the family
  omits **1** as surely as it omits 0; closed mode drew one repair set and
  claimed a finite subcover of `[0,1]` that missed an endpoint. Two green sets
  now, one at each end. Self-check 4 was worse than the canvas: its distractor
  "At the point 1, which no `O_x` contains" was keyed **wrong** and is true. The
  options were rewritten so exactly one is correct.
- **an-12 Problem 3.** `g_a(x) = x^a sin(1/x)` with `a = 3/2, 5/2` is undefined
  for `x < 0`, so "differentiable on ℝ" cannot hold. Abbott's own statement has
  this gap — verified verbatim on printed 136 — so his wording is quoted and a
  note fixes the reading as `|x|^a`, which is odd and therefore transfers the
  `x > 0` work to `x < 0` for free.
- **an-13 Problem 2(b).** The worked start built a moving spike, observed that
  its limit is the zero function, then said "amend the construction" and offered
  two sketches without carrying either out. Replaced with an explicit
  construction: `T_k` of height `k` centred at `2⁻ᵏ` with support half-width
  `2⁻⁽ᵏ⁺²⁾`, disjoint because `5/8 < 3/4`, and `gₙ = Σ₁ⁿ Tₖ`. Continuity comes
  from finiteness of the partial sum; pointwise convergence is trivial because
  each `x` meets at most one spike.
- **an-14 Self-check 2.** The keyed answer named the space of *bounded*
  functions and was right, but the explanation asserted an unrestricted
  equivalence. `fₙ(x) = x + 1/n → x` on ℝ converges uniformly with no bounded
  function in sight. The explanation now states the exact condition: uniform
  convergence to `f` is metric convergence within the set at finite sup-distance
  *from f*.
- **an-14 Problem 6.** Completeness of `C[0,1]` under the sup metric was
  attributed to Theorem 6.2.5 alone. 6.2.5 produces a uniform limit; it says
  nothing about continuity, and completeness needs the limit back inside the
  space. Theorem 6.2.6 is the missing step — both verified on printed 160.

**Did not reproduce:** CodeRabbit's outside-diff comment on `an-05.md` lines
194–196 describes a four-part construction problem with a miscounted number of
impossible cases. That file is 153 lines long and contains no such problem; the

Two of these repairs — an-06's Definition 2.4.1 and an-14's Theorem 6.2.6 — were
